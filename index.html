GIFT: Gradient Informed Filtering for Distillation Augmented Reasoning RL

Shijie Li, Yilin Gao, Tieyuan Chen, Yun Luo, Weiyao Lin, Hang Yu

Training large language models for complex reasoning tasks relies on two complementary signals: sparse, outcome-grounded reinforcement learning (RL) for correctness, and dense, token-level distillation for guidance. However, complementarity in supervision does not guarantee compatibility in optimization; imitation-focused distillation can conflict with the outcome-driven RL objective, and existing hybrids lack a principled resolution. We propose \textbf{GIFT} (\textbf{G}radient-\textbf{I}nformed \textbf{F}il\textbf{T}ering for Distillation-Augmented Reasoning RL), a novel method that directly addresses the allocation problem of which teacher updates to apply. GIFT performs a token-level geometric analysis, decomposing the distillation gradient into a component \textbf{parallel} to the RL gradient, which measures first-order conflict and provides a \textbf{safety} filter, and a component \textbf{orthogonal} to it, which quantifies novel directional guidance and provides a measure of \textbf{value}. This decomposition yields a principled scoring rule to rank token-level guidance. We combine this with a progressive \textbf{coverage annealing} schedule that shrinks the budget of tokens receiving distillation, ensuring that the most conflicting updates are discarded first while the most complementary ones are retained at full strength. This facilitates a smooth transition from dense, geometrically-filtered guidance to a pure, outcome-grounded objective at convergence. Extensive experiments on mathematical and logical reasoning benchmarks show that GIFT consistently outperforms strong baselines across multiple model scales, achieving relative gains of up to 9.5\% on difficult mathematical reasoning tasks. Code: https://github.com/lilifree/GIFT

RoleFlow: Self-Evolving Multi-Role Agentic Collaboration via Structural Adaptation

Yi Ge, Pan Wang, Xuehao Cui, Zian Zeng, Xu Hu, Heng Huang

Large language model (LLM) based multi-agent systems often rely on predefined collaboration structures, limiting their adaptability to evolving task requirements. Although recent approaches enable dynamic orchestration, trajectory-level rewards they apply assign one outcome to each decision, ofen struggling to identify the contributions of individual structural changes. We propose RoleFlow, a self-evolving multi-role agentic system that refines collaboration structures through feedback-driven atomic edits and on-demand role synthesis. To make structural adaptation learnable, we introduce Single-Edit Counterfactual Policy Optimization (SECPO), which evaluates each edit against its no-edit counterpart at the same chain state and assigns targeted credit to edit-specific tokens. Across seven benchmarks spanning four domains, RoleFlow achieves the optimal average performance, surpassing the strongest competitor by 4.7 points. It leads on all three multi-hop QA benchmarks by 3.2-7.5 F1 points and also shows supremacy on mathematical reasoning, web interaction, and code generation. Further analyses demonstrate cross-benchmark transferability, training-data efficiency, and cross-executor generalization.

Sharpness-aware Orthogonalized Momentum Algorithms

Geetika, Shivani Singh Chauhan, Gauranshi Gupta, Anant Jain, Kaushik Kalyanaraman, Bapi Chatterjee

Adversarial sharpness penalty per iteration of a base optimizer, known as sharpness-aware minimization (SAM), is an accepted technique to improve generalization in deep learning. Recently, Muon has emerged as a dominant optimization algorithm for training large language models (LLMs). It achieves lower sample complexity for heavy-tailed gradient updates - a phenomenon common in LLM training. However, sharpness penalties with Muon updates are yet largely unexplored in the literature. Moreover, the behavior of SAM under heavy-tailed noise is unknown both theoretically and empirically. A potential reason for this gap is the additional cost of extra gradient computation per iteration for penalty perturbations in SAM, which becomes prohibitive for LLMs. This paper introduces Sharpness-aware Orthogonalized Momentum Algorithm (SOMA) to combine a computationally efficient and theoretically grounded perturbation scheme with the Muon algorithm. The key idea is to use stale (purely) stochastic gradient noise for adversarial perturbations. Theoretically, we show that SOMA's sample complexity is invariant to the perturbation direction. We provide explorations and insights into SOMA's robustness to heavy-tailed gradient noise. We extensively explore SOMA's efficacy in (a) mitigating quality degradation due to post-training quantization, and (b) catastrophic forgetting during fine-tuning of LLMs.

CoPiT: A conditional Position-induced Transformer for aerodynamic field prediction on unstructured meshes

Junfeng Chen, Yongchen Fan, Zhonghua Qiao, Kailiang Wu

High-fidelity Reynolds-averaged Navier--Stokes (RANS) simulations remain a standard tool for aerodynamic analysis, yet each solve on an unstructured mesh is computationally expensive, making many-query tasks such as design exploration, optimization, and multi-condition evaluation costly. Neural operators provide mesh-flexible surrogates for mapping geometry and operating conditions to flow fields; however, in many existing architectures, global parameters such as the freestream state and design variables are concatenated with pointwise inputs, leaving their influence on intermediate features implicit. This paper proposes CoPiT, a \textbf{Co}nditional \textbf{P}osition-induced \textbf{T}ransformer that builds on the Position-induced Transformer (PiT) as its geometric backbone and injects a global condition vector through multi-stage feature-wise linear modulation (FiLM), thereby making parametric modulation explicit while preserving geometry-based attention weights. The model is evaluated on two-dimensional AirfRANS airfoil fields, including scarce-data and extrapolation regimes, and on three-dimensional BlendedNet++ blended-wing-body surfaces. On the AirfRANS full-data test set, CoPiT reduces surface and volume losses by approximately 54\% and 24\% relative to Transolver, respectively, and achieves the lowest overall normalized test MSE among the compared baselines on BlendedNet++. Matched ablations show that FiLM conditioning generally improves upon input concatenation, while the preferred placement is task-dependent. CoPiT thus provides a practical operator-learning surrogate for parametric aerodynamics on unstructured meshes.

Discovery of Singularities in Partial Differential Equations Using Adaptive Numerical Discretizations

Allen Yang, Lucas Amoudruz, Petros Koumoutsakos

We compute self-similar singularity profiles of partial differential equations (PDEs) using adaptive numerical discretizations based on optimizing a discrete loss (ODIL). The fields are represented in overlapping local Chebyshev patches joined with a mapped asymptotic patch. After factoring out the leading power laws of solution ansatzes, their limiting amplitudes at infinity are determined by the optimization, eliminating domain truncation and prescribed far-field conditions. Adaptive refinement is guided by Chebyshev-coefficient decay, and the nonlinear least-squares problem is solved using automatically differentiated Gauss--Newton iterations with Levenberg--Marquardt damping. The method achieves O(10^{-14})-level accuracy for Burgers profiles, residuals of O(10^{-14}) to O(10^{-10}) for the generalized De Gregorio family, and of O(10^{-8}) in a local region for the two-dimensional Boussinesq equations. These results demonstrate that ODIL can identify singularity profiles for PDEs with high accuracy using adaptive numerical methods, complementing ongoing efforts that rely on artificial intelligence.

Accordion: Elastic-Depth Inference via Fixed-Prefix Layer Looping

Yudong Zhang, Wei Ding, Kaili Zheng, Yuchen Ding, Zhenyu Zhang

Large language models serve applications with diverse inference budgets, but training and maintaining separate models for each budget incurs substantial costs. Recent Looped Transformers enable flexible inference computation through parameter reuse, but maintaining strong performance across different inference budgets remains an open challenge. We introduce Accordion, a mixture-of-experts-based model that supports five inference budgets through fixed-prefix layer looping. Each budget follows a pre-defined execution path, while the minimum budget retains the unlooped model. Joint pretraining samples one budget per micro-batch and optimizes its next-token prediction loss without additional cross-budget consistency objectives. We further preserve these inference budgets through supervised fine-tuning and reinforcement learning, where parameter-group merging and budget-matched multi-teacher on-policy distillation integrate three domain experts into one model. After 3.6T tokens of pretraining, increasing inference depth improves Accordion-6B-A0.5B's five-category macro-average across 37 tasks by 3.01 percentage points, with the middle budget capturing 89% of this gain using half as many additional layer executions. The final model surpasses each domain expert on its corresponding task-and-budget average, while achieving strong instruction-following performance across all budgets and retaining depth-dependent gains in mathematical reasoning and code generation.

A Transformer-based Approach for Real-time Multi-agent Path Planning with Varying Initial/Final Conditions

Qian Zhang, Shanqing Liu, Jerome Darbon, George Karniadakis

We address the problem of real-time multi-agent path planning under prescribed initial and final configurations, formulated as a high-dimensional optimal control problem subject to inter-agent collision avoidance and obstacle constraints. Classical solvers based on the Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman equation or Pontryagin's maximum principle (PMP) become computationally intractable as the number of agents grows, due to the curse of dimensionality and the non-convexity introduced by interaction constraints. We propose OCFormer, a transformer-based neural network that parameterizes the inverse of a coordinate transformation mapping a simple, analytically tractable latent space, in which agents evolve independently under a decoupled Hamiltonian, to the physical space of the coupled, interacting system. The model is trained by minimizing a physics-informed loss comprising the Hamiltonian ODE residual from the PMP and a boundary matching term, without requiring pre-computed trajectory data. The self-attention mechanism endows OCFormer with permutation equivariance over agents, a structural symmetry that prior symplectic network approaches do not natively enforce. When trained on a distribution of boundary conditions, the amortized OCFormer produces near-optimal trajectories for unseen initial and final configurations in a single forward pass ($\sim$220ms), enabling real-time inference without retraining. We further introduce a lightweight transfer learning framework that adapts a pretrained OCFormer to out-of-distribution perturbations in seconds via L-BFGS fine-tuning. Numerical experiments on two- and three-dimensional multi-agent navigation with up to 128 agents demonstrate the scalability and efficiency of the proposed approach.

Reinforcement Learning for Global-Local Control of Dynamic Networks with Applications to Systemic Risk

Nils Detering, Florian Grell, Thilo Meyer-Brandis

We propose a reinforcement learning framework for solving control problems on dynamic networks, where control decisions consist of a combination of graph-level (global) and node-level (local) actions. To exploit the underlying network structure in a policy-gradient algorithm, we introduce a message-passing neural network architecture with separate global and local policy heads. The architecture ensures that the resulting policies satisfy natural permutation symmetries and transparency constraints. Our main theoretical contribution is an analysis of the robustness and regularity properties of the proposed architecture. We derive explicit Lipschitz bounds with respect to both parameter and input perturbations and use these estimates to establish convergence guarantees for a projected policy-gradient algorithm. The resulting constants reveal how architectural choices and graph characteristics influence sensitivity to input perturbations and the convergence behavior of gradient-based optimization methods. As an application, we study the management of systemic risk in financial networks, where a regulator allocates capital interventions across interconnected institutions. Numerical experiments demonstrate that the proposed reinforcement learning architecture outperforms standard architectures based on feedforward neural networks while the learned policies fulfill desirable symmetry and transparency constraints. The algorithm scales to large networks and effectively combines global budget decisions with local allocation policies by exploiting the underlying graph structure.

EPIC: Rethinking Trust Regions for Autoregressive LLM Policies via Exact-KL Prefix Importance-Ratio Clipping

Chengxi Li, Hanlin Wu, Zhicheng Cai, Huan-ang Gao, Wei-Ying Ma, Jingjing Liu

Reinforcement learning (RL) based on PPO-style clipping has become an effective approach to improving the reasoning capabilities of large language models (LLMs). However, standard token-level clipping measures policy change only through the conditional probability of the current token, while the prefix conditioning context is itself generated by the policy. This mismatch becomes especially consequential in long-response and off-policy training. We reformulate autoregressive generation as a finite-horizon Markov decision process and derive its performance-difference identity under behavior-policy sampling. The resulting change of measure identifies the cumulative likelihood ratio of the generated prefix as the exact importance weight for each stepwise policy-improvement term. Starting from the monotonic-improvement principle of trust-region policy optimization, we then construct a prefix-level trust region using the exact reverse-KL coordinate $r-1-\log r$, whose behavior-policy expectation equals the corresponding prefix-distribution KL divergence. This yields EPIC, a PPO-style sequence-clipping method that directly regulates prefix-level policy drift without relying on a local quadratic approximation or expert-routing replay. Experiments on off-policy reinforcement learning with a sparse mixture-of-experts language model show that EPIC consistently outperforms standard and sequence-level clipping baselines across mathematical-reasoning benchmarks, while remaining competitive with routing-replay-based alternatives. Code is available at https://github.com/LiChengxi666/EPIC.

Contextual Decision Making: Predictive or Generative

L. Jeff Hong, Weihuan Huang, Xinyao Li

Contextual decision making uses observed covariates to guide decisions under uncertainty. Two natural approaches are predict-then-optimize (PtO), which learns the conditional expected objective, and generate-then-optimize (GtO), which learns conditional uncertainty and optimizes using generated scenarios. We study when each approach is preferable by examining how the choice of learning target affects statistical accuracy and optimization tractability. Although PtO learns a narrower target, our regret bounds show how differences in smoothness can offset GtO's additional dimensional burden. Exploiting smoothness also raises an optimization tradeoff: within the class of nonnegative-weight PtO methods studied, a lower bound limits the gains from additional smoothness, while more flexible methods may introduce signed weights and lose the guarantee of preserving convexity. GtO preserves scenario-wise convexity, but requires learning a richer object and generating scenarios for optimization. Synthetic experiments and a real case study illustrate how these tradeoffs vary with data availability and problem structure. Together with the scope for reusing a model across tasks, our findings provide practical guidance for choosing between the approaches without identifying a universal winner.

Minimax Rates for imperfect Physics-Informed Regression

Nathan Doumèche

Physics-informed machine learning incorporates physical knowledge into data-driven models, often through a partial differential equation (PDE) that the target is assumed to satisfy. However, how much this knowledge improves statistical performance remains unclear, especially when the physical model is imperfect. Since satisfying a PDE implies being differentiable, a natural benchmark for physics-informed regression is the best achievable performance when smoothness is the only prior information about the target function. We show that there is a characteristic threshold for the modeling error above which the physical prior cannot improve the convergence rate. The form of this threshold is common to every linear PDE and certain nonlinear PDEs. Below it, the gain depends on the equation type. For classes of elliptic and hyperbolic PDEs with constant coefficients, we establish convergence rates that improve continuously as the modeling error decreases, and prove their minimax optimality up to logarithmic factors. With exact physics, the rates correspond to learning in dimension d-1 rather than d, reflecting the freedom left by unknown boundary or initial data. We also give conditions for computing the estimator with near-linear complexity and show how to tune its hyperparameters without knowing the modeling error.

SemantEq: Faithful Bidirectional Equivalence of Formal Statements

Xinrui Li, Pawan Sasanka Ammanamanchi, Siddharth Bhat, Rianna Jamdar, Yongxi Lin, Stella Biderman

Statement autoformalization translates mathematics from natural language into a proof assistant such as Lean 4, and models now do it at scale. As provers get stronger, the burden of correctness shifts from the proof to the statement, that is, to whether the formal statement faithfully translates its source. Given a trusted reference formalization, the natural test is logical equivalence, and it proves too much. $P \leftrightarrow Q$ holds trivially for any two statements $P$ and $Q$ that the library proves, because a prover can prove each separately and pass through $P \leftrightarrow \mathrm{True} \leftrightarrow Q$. The stronger the prover, the more unrelated pairs pass. Earlier methods restrict the prover and check that each proof names the other statement. We introduce SemantEq, a more principled relation that instead requires each statement to turn into the other only through a chain of allowed steps, such as logical rearrangement and rewriting with the library's own equations. Our programs judge a proof by the proof term Lean checks, not by its tactic script. SemantEq-Eval, a search without a model, reaches 100% precision and 65.6% recall on the ProofNetVerif test split under our adjudicated labels, against 95.8% and 61.8% for BEq+, and an 8.9-fold increase in its CPU time adds at most three pairs. A language model finds more proofs, but under two of four prompts it proves both directions of about two in five of the non-equivalent pairs. SemantEq-ProofAudit audits each model-written proof under a policy derived from the relation and accepts none of the 104 non-equivalent pairs, and it raises recall on 407 equivalent pairs from 65.4% to 81.1% under the best prompt. Separating the generation of equivalence proofs from their acceptance lets a stronger prover raise recall without changing what an acceptance means. We release code and data at: https://github.com/hlxr89/SemantEq

Covariance-Informed Ensemble Score Filtering: Embedding Localized Covariance Geometry into Score-Based Transport

Background-error covariance models are central to atmospheric data assimilation because they spread partial observational information across space, variables, and dynamically coupled scales. Ensemble and variational methods exploit this covariance geometry efficiently, but their standard analysis formulations are largely built around Gaussian or locally quadratic approximations. Score-based ensemble filtering provides a framework for nonlinear distributional transport, but existing Ensemble Score Filter (EnSF) formulations lack an intrinsic covariance-mediated pathway for propagating likelihood information from observed to unobserved state components. We introduce a covariance-informed ensemble score filter (CI-EnSF) that embeds localized forecast-error covariance geometry directly into the forward diffusion and reverse transport. The proposed forward diffusion is covariance-shaped in standardized state space and becomes isotropic under the corresponding control-variable transformation. Experiments with Lorenz-96 and surface quasi-geostrophic (SQG) dynamics show a clear regime dependence. For linear observations, a well-tuned LETKF is more accurate, while CI-EnSF maintains stable cycling and effective covariance-mediated information propagation. Under strongly nonlinear observations and under climatological initialization, CI-EnSF achieves lower analysis error and greater cycling robustness than Gaussian ensemble benchmarks such as the LETKF, with improvements extending across spatial scales in the nonlinear SQG experiment. These results suggest that the principal benefit of CI-EnSF arises from combining nonlinear distributional transport with the mature covariance geometry used in conventional data assimilation.
