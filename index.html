A Validation-Driven Bilevel Optimization Method for Mesh-Free PDE Solvers and Its Error Analysis

Yongcun Song, Shangzhi Zeng, Jin Zhang, Lvgang Zhang

Mesh-free partial differential equation (PDE) solvers construct discrete systems from residuals at sampled points. These solvers fit coefficients in a parameterized trial representation and select solver-design parameters governing the representation, training-residual weighting, and regularization. These parameters strongly affect solution accuracy, yet existing selection strategies are largely tailored to particular solver classes. To address this limitation, we formulate a unified validation-driven bilevel optimization framework applicable across different solver classes, in which the lower level fits coefficients using training collocation residuals, while the upper level selects solver-design parameters using held-out validation residuals. For nonlinear PDEs, however, solving the nonlinear lower-level problem to high accuracy is costly, while the adjoint system is typically ill-conditioned and indefinite. To address this issue, we propose an efficient implicit hypergradient method that approximates the lower-level response using finite, warm-started damped Gauss-Newton (GN) updates. For the adjoint solve, we use the damped GN matrix to compute an initial adjoint and to precondition a finite number of minimum residual iterations. To quantify the errors introduced by these approximations, we derive local hypergradient error bounds that separate the contributions of the finite lower-level solve, adjoint damping, and the final adjoint residual. Combining this analysis with validation sampling bounds and residual stability, we relate validation residuals to PDE solution accuracy and establish error bounds for the computed PDE solution. Numerical experiments on several nonlinear PDEs, including a high-dimensional problem, demonstrate the accuracy and efficiency of the proposed method across Kansa, random feature and extreme learning machine, and physics-informed neural network solvers.

A Calculus Gradient: Child Poverty, Mathematics Preparation, and First-Year College Performance at a Hispanic-Serving University

Juan B. Gutiérrez

The San Antonio metropolitan area has one of the highest poverty rates among large U.S. metropolitan areas. Within Bexar County, where the city is located, child poverty varies widely across school districts. We link entrants of the city's public research university, a broad-access Hispanic-Serving Institution, to their sending schools and districts, and examine their preparation scores and first-year outcomes. Across the nation's school districts, enrollment in widely taken core courses (Algebra II, Geometry, Biology) shows little relation to child poverty, while enrollment in Calculus and Advanced Placement mathematics and science is far lower where child poverty is higher, nationally and within Bexar County. Among linked entrants, mathematics preparation differs with the school and district. Entrants with stronger mathematics preparation earn higher first-year grades in nearly every subject. Most entrants who do not return after the first year had unsuccessful courses, and mathematics is the largest single source of those outcomes. Among students who change major, mathematics is one of the subjects with grades furthest below their own averages. Within the same course and term, larger mathematics sections go with more unsuccessful outcomes for entrants with lower mathematics preparation, which identifies a hypothesis for testing smaller sections. Recorded between Fall 2014 and Fall 2019, these outcomes offer a reference point for change after the pandemic, test-optional admissions, and generative artificial intelligence. The analysis is reproducible; adapting it elsewhere requires local definitions and linkage validation. All results are associations among the students of one institution; establishing causes would require a designed study.

From these observations, we derive a workflow for recurring proof patterns and practical guidance for using AI agents while preserving mathematical soundness. Our results characterize both the practical value and the current limitations of AI-assisted formalization for trustworthy scientific computing.

Beyond PINNs: Boundary-Enriched Holomorphic Networks for Laplace Problems

Andrei Vlad Nicula, Kalp Shah, Robert-Stefan Sofroni, Mihnea Andrei Bârsan, Agneya Bhardwaj, Jan Jelínek

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) solve partial differential equations by penalizing their residual, but this formulation can be difficult to optimize for solutions with singular behaviour. We focus specifically on the two-dimensional Laplace equation, whose strong analytical structure allows the governing equation to be enforced directly in the neural representation. We compare a hybrid harmonic-neural model with a holomorphic neural network whose output is harmonic by construction and therefore requires only boundary training. We further enrich the holomorphic model with singular functions chosen according to the interior angle and boundary conditions at each junction of boundary segments. On six singular benchmarks, the enriched model is the most accurate of the neural models we compare. On the four with a closed-form solution, however, that solution lies in the span of the enrichment terms, and a least-squares fit of these terms alone is exact to rounding error. On Motz's problem and the cracked beam, the neural core reduces the relative error of the recovered leading singular coefficient from about $10^{-2}$, for a least-squares fit with the same four terms, to about $10^{-6}$, and on four variants whose solution has a smooth part outside the span, the network is $4\times10^{4}$ to $2\times10^{7}$ times more accurate than a fit of its singular terms alone; classical least-squares fits with more singular terms, or with harmonic polynomials and rational poles, are more accurate still. Our results demonstrate how exploiting problem-specific analytical structure can provide an alternative to base and residual PINNs for challenging scientific computing problems.

Nearly Minimax-Optimal Sample Complexity of Natural Policy Gradient with a Matching Information-Theoretic Lower Bound

Asha Barua, Sajad Khodadadian

A fundamental question in reinforcement learning is how many sampled transitions are needed to learn a near-optimal policy, and whether direct policy optimization can attain this statistical limit. To answer this question, we consider natural policy gradient (NPG), a widely used policy optimization method. We study tabular finite-horizon Markov decision processes (MDPs) with known rewards and unknown horizon-dependent transition kernels, accessed through a generative model. Our sample-based NPG algorithms update the policy by backward sweeps and do not estimate transition probabilities. Under three different batch-size and step size schedules, we establish last-iterate guarantees with sample complexity $\widetilde O(SAH^4/\varepsilon^2)$, where $S$, $A$, and $H$ denote the number of states, the number of actions, and the horizon length, respectively. In this setting, horizon-dependent transition kernels contribute a factor of $H$ to our transition-query bound. Our information-theoretic lower bound of $Ω(SAH^4/\varepsilon^2)$ shows that the resulting $H^4$ dependence is unavoidable in the worst case, even for agents that select their queries adaptively. To the best of our knowledge, these are the first last-iterate guarantees for NPG whose sample complexity matches an information-theoretic lower bound up to logarithmic factors. Consequently, our results demonstrate that direct policy-space optimization through NPG can achieve the fundamental statistical limits of reinforcement learning while guaranteeing the performance of the final policy. Furthermore, for known kernels, we prove that exact NPG with a constant step size $η=1/H$ requires $Ω(H^2/\varepsilon)$ iterations in the worst case, matching the known upper bound for that step size.

Congestion-Aware Adaptive Routing for LLM Classification

Yuan Guo, Stefanus Jasin, Chen-An Lin

Organizations use large language models (LLMs) to classify jobs that differ in uncertainty and the cost of errors. Querying additional models may improve accuracy, but it also incurs fees and increases congestion at shared servers. We study congestion-aware adaptive routing (CAAR) policies that adapt model selection and stopping decisions to observed outputs while accounting for the steady-state congestion they generate. The objective is to minimize the long-run average cost of misclassification penalties, query fees, and holding costs. We can represent CAAR policies as a mixture of decision trees, and derive exact holding costs for first-come-first-served servers with exponential service times and processor-sharing servers with general service times. We formulate a convex optimization problem with unique optimal model loads. Congestion prices capture the marginal system-wide holding costs of additional queries and guide routing and stopping decisions through a Bayesian dynamic program. We characterize optimal stopping decisions and identify models whose information does not justify their cost. A column-generation algorithm computes optimal CAAR policies and provides an explicit optimality-gap bound. We also bound the additional cost of limiting cascade depth relative to full-depth CAAR. Finally, under a common query-depth limit, cascades with a predetermined model order can be arbitrarily more costly than optimal CAAR, even when the order is randomized and stopping decisions adapt to observed outputs. Numerical experiments distinguish the benefits of adaptive model selection and congestion-aware design.

Expressive Shallow Networks for Physics-Informed Learning: Why Curvature-Aware Optimization and FP64 Are Not Enough

Zachary Morrow, Yucheng Fu, Pratanu Roy, Michael Penwarden

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) often struggle with high-frequency and high-order partial differential equations (PDEs) due to optimization stiffness and spectral bias, despite possessing sufficient approximation capacity. Existing remedies, including modified feature representations, domain decomposition, higher floating-point precision, and curvature-aware optimizers, can improve convergence but largely act as workarounds for optimization difficulties arising from deep architectures rather than addressing their underlying source. We investigate Physics-Informed Shallow Universal Polynomial Networks (PI-SUPNs), which replace deep nonlinear compositions with learnable polynomial expansions, retaining high approximation capacity within a shallow computational graph. On a high-frequency Helmholtz benchmark, PI-SUPNs are the only architecture to successfully recover the solution, while all competing models fail even under FP64 precision and curvature-aware optimization. However, experiments on higher vibrational modes of a fourth-order Euler-Bernoulli beam reveal that architectural improvements alone are insufficient: accurate solutions require the combination of PI-SUPNs, FP64 precision, and curvature-aware optimization. Additionally, Allen-Cahn experiments show that PI-SUPNs achieve lower solution and derivative errors at reduced computational cost, with more favorable loss landscapes than competing architectures. We also establish quasi-local approximation properties and spline-based convergence bounds for SUPNs. Our results show that shallow network architectures are an important mechanism for improving physics-informed trainability, but accurate solutions to the most challenging PDEs can still require FP64 precision and curvature-aware optimization.

Xi Chen, Yuze Chen, Baichuan Mo, Jie Ying, Yuan Zhou

Designing simple inventory policies becomes increasingly difficult as operational features and system configurations expand. We develop an Agentic Inventory-Policy Discovery System based on explicitly defined simple operational formula (SOF) policy and template classes. Large language models (LLMs) revise symbolic structures, while a numerical engine fits parameters separately for each instance and evaluates the induced policies. Multicriteria parent selection, state-specific operational diagnostics, and reusable symbolic motifs guide structural evolution. A three-stage progression develops shared templates across instances and configurations with varying state dimensions. Experiments on 15,600 instances spanning 50 demand distributions cover lead times, perishability, their combination, and lead times with limited customer patience. The discovered templates improve aggregate empirical performance over evaluated SOF benchmarks and are competitive with policies requiring more elaborate online computations. For a lost-sales system with a three-period lead time, we develop an average-cost decomposition and prove that, under specified demand and cost conditions, a capped dual-shortfall specialization of a discovered policy strictly outperforms every policy in the considered capped base-stock benchmark class. This analysis links the performance advantage to the discovered replenishment mechanism. Together, these results highlight the potential of agentic discovery to generate simple inventory policies with strong empirical performance and identify operational mechanisms amenable to rigorous theoretical analysis.

Finite-difference neural policy iteration for high-dimensional degenerate Hamilton--Jacobi equations

Hee Jun Yang, Yeoneung Kim

We develop a finite-difference neural policy iteration method for high-dimensional degenerate Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman and Isaacs equations. Artificial viscosity yields a monotone finite-difference operator, and neural networks approximate the linear equations of semi-discrete policy iteration using local stencil values. This avoids global tensor-grid storage. For measurable feedbacks, we bound the propagation of evaluation, terminal, gradient, and control optimization errors by a geometrically weighted sum. Discrete comparison, consistency, and vanishing viscosity relate the approximation to the original degenerate equation. On coupled manufactured problems with rank-deficient diffusion, the method achieves lower mean errors than direct physics-informed neural networks at every tested dimension from five to fifty under equal training time. At fifty dimensions and $900$ seconds, the mean relative $L^2$ error over three paired seeds is about $40\%$ lower. Two-dimensional refinement attains $0.297\%$ error against the original degenerate solution, with independent grid references separating approximation error from viscosity bias. In a twenty-dimensional game with finite control sets, reusing policies reduces the measured time to a relative error of $5\%$ by $24.3\%$ compared with direct minimization of the same discrete residual with fresh samples at every update.

The frequent absence of ground-truth knowledge or labels complicates verification of generative AI outputs. To address this issue, we propose a trustworthiness framework grounded in model-internal confidence calibrated against an external expert reference. We estimate per-token trustworthiness of a student generative model as the statistical typicality of each generated token relative to an expert model's distribution, where the expert is used as a distributional reference rather than as a source of factual verification labels. Evaluated across BF program generation, mathematical problem solving, and medical question answering, combined calibrator and traditional indicators provide complementary reliability signal which could be used to build detectors of untrustworthy outputs and hallucinations. Remarkably, our experiments show that these detectors are transferable across domains, although transferability is not always symmetric.

Hierarchical randomized neural networks for efficiently and accurately finding multiple solutions of semilinear elliptic equations

Jingying Hu, Jiao Li, Yufeng Xu, Jinyong Ying

Being able to find multiple solutions of a semilinear elliptic equation is a fundamental problem in scientific computing. In this paper, fully taking the advantages of randomized neural networks, we propose a new efficient and accurate method for identifying potentially as many solutions as possible. The new method is constructed from three aspects--initialization, optimization, a technique for acceleration and improving accuracy, two of which are innovative developments. Firstly, based on the basis functions of a randomized network and the sampling points, a characteristic matrix is introduced so that its singular value decomposition can be used to determine weakly variant search directions, followed by a newly developed algorithm to generate effective initial candidates. Then the good deflated Gauss-Newton method [see {\em A. Riley, et al., SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 47~(5) (2025) A2963-A2987}] using an exponential deflation operator is adapted as the optimization to search for all multiple solutions. Local error estimate of a particular solution under some proper condition is given in terms of the neurons in the hidden layer and the sampling points for this newly designed method. Furthermore, to accelerate the computation and improve the solution accuracy, a new multilevel method based on hierarchical networks is designed. Without using any {\em a priori} information, several numerical tests show together with the multilevel method, the new method can not only accurately find multiple solutions with relative errors reaching $10^{-16}$ only in less than one-tenth computational cost for the best case, but also preserve the inherent symmetries of multiple solutions.

Symmetries, conservation laws, series solutions and singularity formation for the spherically symmetric Euler-Poisson equations in mass coordinates

Adnan Shamaoon

Written in Lagrangian mass coordinates, the spherically symmetric Euler-Poisson equations for a polytropic gas become a single second-order equation for the radius $r(t,m)$ of the shell enclosing mass $m$, with adiabatic index $γ>1$ and entropy profile $S(m)$. We determine all point symmetries of this equation. Apart from time translation there is at most one scaling, except at the critical index $γ=4/3$, where a scaling exists for every entropy profile; these scalings are the entropy-fixed stabilisers of the two-parameter scaling group of the non-isentropic system, and we give the exact symmetry defect when $γ\neq4/3$. Noether's theorem singles out the profiles $S=Km^{14-10γ}$, for which a scaling is variational and gives a conservation law beyond energy; otherwise the scalings yield virial identities. We construct exact collapse solutions and reduce the self-similar problem to one ordinary differential equation. Using the Briot-Bouquet theorem we prove that the regular centre of self-similar and homologous collapse carries a convergent analytic expansion with free central amplitude, and we derive a Puiseux expansion at the stellar surface whose exponent is the polytropic index $1/(γ-1)$. In mass coordinates a density singularity is a zero of $r_m$ or of $r$; for the first kind we prove a Hopf-type fold law and an exact shell-crossing criterion for free-falling dust. Finally, linear perturbations of the critical Einstein-de Sitter collapse separate exactly into spherical Bessel profiles, with exponents solving $3κ^2+κ-2+μ=0$. All identities were checked by computer algebra, and the numerical statements rest on convergence studies of a second-order Lagrangian scheme, including a collapse with a free surface.

Verifiable On-Policy Distillation

Juntong Shi, Stefano Ermon, Jure Leskovec, Minkai Xu

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has become a powerful approach for improving language-model reasoning, but its supervision is inherently sparse: a sequence-level verifier reward is propagated only through sampled trajectories and provides no direct signal for alternative actions. On-policy distillation (OPD) offers a complementary form of supervision by providing dense teacher feedback over the full next-token distribution, but this signal is not grounded in verified task outcomes. Simply co-optimizing the two objectives does not couple these signals: verifier feedback remains sparse and does not inform the dense teacher supervision. We introduce Verifiable On-Policy Distillation (VOPD), which transfers sparse verifier feedback into dense, verifier-grounded supervision. Our key observation is that the implicit-reward view of OPD places teacher supervision and verifier feedback in a common reward space while retaining a reward signal available for every next-token action. Under this view, VOPD projects verifier preference onto the teacher's implicit-reward direction and Rao--Blackwellizes the resulting signal over the full vocabulary, propagating verifier-grounded supervision to actions that were never sampled. The resulting objective can also be interpreted as adaptively extrapolating the distillation target according to teacher--verifier agreement. Across two teacher--student configurations and eight mathematical reasoning benchmarks, VOPD consistently improves both final performance and learning efficiency over vanilla OPD and other on-policy distillation baselines. Our code is available at https://github.com/juntongshi48/VOPD.

We also derive convergence rates for polynomial inverse-temperature schedules up to and including linear growth, extending the marginal KL bound obtained by mirror-descent analysis at fixed temperature.
