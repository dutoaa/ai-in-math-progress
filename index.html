EvoOPD: Evolving Privileged Information Strategies for Black-Box On-Policy Distillation

Ximo Zhu, Ruiqi Liu, Yi Han, Zhengan Wu, Gengsheng Li, ZiAn Wang

Proprietary language models exhibit strong reasoning and coding capabilities, but their output distributions are inaccessible, making fine-grained distillation methods that rely on teacher logits difficult to apply directly to black-box models. We observe that rich textual feedback from a black-box teacher can be treated as privileged information (PI) and converted, through a PI-conditioned privileged teacher, into dense supervision signals on the student's own trajectories. However, what PI should be acquired from the teacher to most effectively support student learning remains unclear. PI strategies that differ in where they focus, what information they request, and how much detail they provide can have substantially different effects on the current student, and their relative effectiveness can further change as training progresses. Based on this observation, we propose Evolving Privileged Information Strategies for Black-Box On-Policy Distillation (EvoOPD), which treats PI acquisition itself as an optimization problem and maintains a PI Strategy Bank that evolves with the student state. EvoOPD uses the measured repair utility of PI on the current student to adapt the focus, information type, and granularity of teacher feedback, allowing the acquired information to continually match the student's learning needs. We systematically evaluate EvoOPD on mathematical reasoning and code generation with Qwen3-1.7B, 4B, and 8B students. EvoOPD achieves the best overall performance in all six model-task settings, improving over the strongest baseline in each setting by an average of 2.14 percentage points in mathematics and 1.30 points in code. Further analysis shows that the relative effectiveness of PI acquisition strategies changes with student state, making a fixed strategy difficult to sustain as the best choice throughout distillation.

The Cost of Mismatch: Noise Amplification in Zeroth-Order Reinforcement Learning

Lianmin Chen, Junbin Qiu, Chenxing Wei, Yao Shu, Kun He

Zeroth-order (ZO) optimization has emerged as a promising approach for memory-efficient large language model (LLM) post-training, yet its reliability in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) remains largely unexplored. Motivated by the distinct data regimes of on-policy RL and fixed-data optimization, we develop a generic variance-theoretic framework to investigate what limits the reliability of ZO gradient estimation. Our analysis reveals that estimation reliability is structurally determined by whether the two finite-difference evaluations are performed on matched data. On-policy reinforcement learning (RL) under the mismatched-data regime produces a $1/μ^2$ oracle-noise amplification that sets an irreducible noise floor as the perturbation scale $μ$ decreases, whereas Boltzmann Targeted SFT (BOLT) under the matched-data regime eliminates this amplification via noise cancellation. We further derive convergence guarantees with explicit constants that cleanly separate the two regimes. These results motivate the ZO-BOLT algorithm, which builds on BOLT's matched-data advantage for reliable ZO optimization of an RLVR-aligned objective. Controlled perturbation-scale sweeps and matched-versus-mismatched end-to-end comparisons validate the predicted effect of data matching. Experiments across three models and two mathematical reasoning benchmarks further show that ZO-BOLT consistently improves over the pretrained baseline while retaining a near-inference memory footprint. The code is publicly available at https://github.com/lianmin-chen/the_cost_of_mismatch.

On the Generalization Limitation of On-Policy Distillation for Post-RL Consolidation

Yichen Wang, Yufa Zhou, Siqi Zhu, Haozheng Luo, Jingzhi Bao, Zhichao Liu

Multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) extends on-policy distillation (OPD) to consolidate capabilities from multiple teachers. Although prior work reports near-perfect in-distribution (IND) distillation performance, we find that this can mask incomplete capability consolidation beyond the supervised distribution. A preliminary study on synthetic reasoning shows that OPD does not fully transfer teachers' generalization capabilities. We then extend this investigation to real-world tasks, evaluating extrapolative and compositional generalization across mathematical reasoning, coding, agent, and long-context capabilities using Qwen3 models. Across extrapolative settings, OPD and MOPD reach 96.04-102.73% of their teachers' IND scores. Yet their out-of-distribution (OOD) scores remain 2.45-16.25% below the base model's. Across three compositional settings, MOPD improves IND performance over the base model, but its performance on composed tasks as OOD remains 18.19-24.34% below that of the best teachers. To understand these gaps, we examine teacher capability and data coverage as two potential bottlenecks. Jointly alleviating these bottlenecks eliminates OOD forgetting in our experiments and suggests the potential for generalization beyond the teacher. (M)OPD also reduces OOD forgetting on average relative to its reinforcement learning (RL) baselines. Analysis of the associated update geometry reveals smaller and more spectrally concentrated updates on average than those of the RL baselines, along with greater shared update structure under MOPD. Motivated by these findings, we propose geometry-based patching and base-preserving replay to improve OOD performance while largely preserving IND performance. Our findings identify a blind spot in capability consolidation via (M)OPD, highlighting the need for algorithms that explicitly support OOD retention and generalization.

The results demonstrate the feasibility of a unified operational framework for heterogeneous foundation-level tasks and clarify the gap between such a framework and a fully integrated mathematical foundation.

Self-Privileged Fine-Tuning

Yangrong Hu, Xueyu Zhou, Yue Wu, Jian Huang

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is a key component of language model post-training, enabling effective task adaptation and serving as an important foundation for subsequent reinforcement learning. Recent methods seek to improve SFT by introducing token-level weights into the training objective, where these weights are typically derived from a model's output distribution at each token position. To better understand how such weighting schemes affect SFT, we analyze their optimization objectives and theoretically characterize the target distributions they induce. Our analysis shows that dynamic weighting methods, such as Dynamic Fine-Tuning (DFT), induce a heavily sharpened distribution, whereas static weighting methods fit a target distribution proportional to the product of the data distribution and a frozen prior. These findings suggest that static weighting provides a more stable training target, while its effectiveness critically depends on the quality of the prior distribution. Building on this observation, we propose Self-Privileged Fine-Tuning (SPFT), which uses a frozen copy of the initial model conditioned on privileged information (such as ground truth for mathematical questions) as a prior to provide guidance for token-level weighting during fine-tuning. Through a Bayesian decomposition, we further show that SPFT reweights the standard static prior according to how informative each target token is for predicting the ground-truth outcome, thereby assigning larger weights to more predictive tokens. Experiments on mathematical reasoning and code generation show that SPFT improves over standard SFT and other baselines across the main settings.

Ante Sikirica, Ivana Lučin, Marta Alvir, Tin Matulja

Foil windsurfing is a sport in which competitors fine-tune their setup to optimize performance under varying environmental conditions. However, such adjustments are typically guided by intuition. This paper presents a methodology for improving hydrofoil design using computational fluid dynamics. First, structured light scanning was used to obtain the geometry of a conventional hydrofoil. Subsequently, five derived designs have been explored, inspired by advancements in airplane wing design. Following this, a preliminary design was selected, and a design of experiments approach was implemented. Generated simulation data were used to train a machine learning surrogate, which is ultimately used in the multi-objective design optimization. While the surrogate-based approach yielded promising results, it was limited to exploring a restricted set of design parameters. To overcome this constraint, additional improvements were attained through adjoint optimization, demonstrating that comparable performance can be achieved with a distinctly different hydrofoil design. Finally, the conventional design was refined through adjoint optimization. Notably, adjoint optimization yielded a 15% increase in efficiency. The results suggest that both proposed approaches can produce novel hydrofoil designs capable of outperforming the original configuration under specific conditions; however, due to its inherent flexibility, the adjoint approach with realistic constraints offers more freedom in the design exploration process.

Controlling Sharpening in LLM Post-Training

Simon Matrenok, Nicolas Menet, Nicolas Flammarion, Andreas Krause

LLM post-training via reinforcement learning has shown consistent improvements in accuracy at the cost of reducing behavioral coverage - a phenomenon known as sharpening that can stall progress on difficult tasks. Repeated sampling objectives such as $\mathrm{pass@k}$ and $\mathrm{max@k}$ are known to mitigate sharpening, but the mechanism behind it remains poorly understood. Moreover, existing approaches for $\mathrm{@k}$ optimization use dedicated algorithms, and no general framework exists for adapting established policy optimization methods from $\mathrm{@}1$ to $\mathrm{@k}$. We derive the optimal policies for KL-regularized $\mathrm{pass@k}$ and $\mathrm{max@k}$ objectives in closed form. Our explicit solutions reveal that $\mathrm{@k}$ optimization naturally redistributes learning pressure based on prompt difficulty, directing it toward harder problems. Crucially, we show that standard $\mathrm{@}1$ objectives achieve these exact same optimal policies through prompt-adaptive regularization or reward transformation. Ultimately, this yields an optimizer-independent explanation for why $\mathrm{@k}$ objectives reduce sharpening on easy tasks while prioritizing success on harder ones. Building on these insights, we introduce $\mathrm{@k}$ Adaptive Pressure for Policy Optimization (KAPPO). Following a one-time reference calibration, KAPPO seamlessly adapts existing $\mathrm{@}1$ methods to $\mathrm{@k}$ via prompt-adaptive regularization or reward transformation, without otherwise altering their training procedures. Experiments with several RL methods on mathematical reasoning and code generation demonstrate that KAPPO provides direct control over sharpening. Crucially, we demonstrate that $\mathrm{@k}$ pressure can be fully realized with single completion training, matching the performance of multi-completion $\mathrm{@k}$ optimizers at a fraction of the cost.

Asymptotic Optimality of $cμ$-Rule for LLM Inference Scheduling under Memory Constraint

Zhenghao Li, Shengbo Wang, Nian Si

Large language model (LLM) inference, the process by which a pre-trained model receives an input prompt and generates output tokens sequentially, has become increasingly important with the widespread deployment of LLM-based applications. As inference workloads continue to scale, GPU memory has emerged as a critical bottleneck: the long input prompts and generation sequences common in agentic and reasoning workloads lead to substantial key-value (KV) cache usage. In this paper, we study the resulting online scheduling problem from a queueing perspective. Requests arrive stochastically with heterogeneous prompt and output lengths, undergo prefill and autoregressive decode, and compete for a finite KV-cache capacity. Unlike classical queueing models, the duration of a processing step depends on both prefill computation and KV-cache I/O, so service capacity is jointly determined by computation, memory access, and memory occupancy. We develop a fluid model that reduces the system to a multiclass single-server queue with effective service rates capturing computation, memory access, and memory capacity. This fluid analysis yields a $cμ$ priority rule, where the effective service rate $μ$ is jointly determined by computational speed, memory bandwidth, and memory capacity. We prove that this rule minimizes cumulative holding cost in the fluid model and, for a finite workload, also minimizes completion time and maximizes full-run output-token throughput. We then construct a feasible scheduling policy for the original finite system and prove that its scaled dynamics converge to the optimal fluid policy. Finally, experiments on a real LLM serving engine using an NVIDIA H800 show that practical implementations of the proposed priority rule substantially reduce average latency while maintaining high throughput relative to existing scheduling policies.

A Universal Policy Wrapper With Guarantees

Georgiy Malaniya, Anton Bolychev, Pavel Osinenko

Many control tasks require driving the system into a prescribed goal set and subsequently keeping it there. The considered setting assumes that a working controller is available that provably reaches and maintains the goal, albeit with suboptimal performance. This controller is referred to as the fallback policy. Conversely, although state-of-the-art reinforcement-learning (RL) algorithms can discover policies with impressive empirical performance, the neural networks at their core act as opaque black boxes learned through data-driven exploration and, as such, provide no theoretical mechanism to guarantee that the system will ever reach---and stay within---the prescribed goal set. This article presents CALF-Wrapper, a method that combines the fallback policy with a pretrained RL base policy and uses the critic estimate associated with the base policy to select between them during deployment. The theoretical analysis establishes that CALF-Wrapper inherits the goal-reaching property of the fallback policy under the stated assumptions and provides probability bounds for the settling time and the maximum distance from the goal set over a prescribed time interval. The experimental study evaluates CALF-Wrapper in four simulated control environments of different complexity. Comparisons include the base policy, fallback policy, and constrained reinforcement-learning methods formulated for the same objective. Sensitivity and ablation studies provide direct hyperparameter recommendations. The experiments show how CALF-Wrapper can provide effective task performance while attaining the maximum possible observed goal-reaching rate. CALF-Wrapper requires no additional dynamics model, online learning, or constrained optimization during execution.

De Rham cohomological architectures for PINNs and PDEs with topological holes

Andrew Gracyk

We attempt to alleviate failure modes of classical physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) in scenarios of topological pathologies corresponding to punctures, holes, poles, and voids by introducing de Rham cohomological augmentations, or Hodge-de Rham PINNs. Vanilla PINNs often encounter pitfalls in hole-based settings due to discontinuous interfaces, nonzero closed path integration, path dependence, correspondence to exact forms via the exterior derivative, etc., therefore we absolve traditional failures via parameterizing a 1-form by the Hodge-Morrey-Friedrichs (HMF) decomposition for manifolds with boundary, drawing a connection to de Rham cohomology such as through the HMF isomorphism theorem. 1-forms can act as representations of scalar-valued PDE solutions through integral operator mappings. We propose performing a PINN-based loss on the parameterized 1-form through an associated PDE, which subsequently solves the potential-based PINN problem and moreover remedies the failures of vanilla PINNs via the Sobolev-regular operator under a Fréchet differentiation interconnection. The operator relationship often has a nontrivial closed-form and often requires integration or a case-by-case analysis, which we address thoroughly in our experiments. When an integrability condition is not met on our losses, we can equip exhaustion functions to train the networks. We demonstrate our methods on the Aharonov-Bohm effect, which, to our understanding, is largely nonexistent in PINN literature. We develop theoretical guarantees of our methods and demonstrate our pathological safeguards on a broad selection of topologically-adverse problems, including scenarios of inexact forms, and those of nontrivial harmonics and coexact terms.

Adaptive Multi Mode: Budget-Aware Routing of Optimizers By Online Landscape Probes

Benjamin Jacob Bodner

The best classical optimizer for a continuous black-box problem depends on its budget and landscape. Existing routers decide from the dimension and budget, past improvement or features fitted to one benchmark, and few test an optimizer's implicit assumptions online. AMM (Adaptive Multi Mode) routes a known budget among quasi-Newton, trust-region, coordinate, multistart and evolution-strategy optimization engines with online probes of curvature, plateaus, separability, kinks, basins and fractal structure. Each probe has a default, a dimension-by-budget validity range and a cost charged to the budget. In its pre-registered development loop on bbob, 32 of 36 predictions held over the last 16 confirmations. AMM was then tested once on held-out bbob instances 101-110 (D=2-40, 10*D-1000*D) against up to seven competitors. It has the highest target share in 382 of 600 function-dimension-budget cells (236 outright, 146 tied) and, by a descriptive posterior rule, is credibly behind in 35, mostly at 10*D and 30*D. At every budget it also leads the controls built from its own engines (the best engine alone, the best engine per dimension and budget, and a greedy schedule), narrowly at 10*D and 30*D, and nevergrad's NgIohTuned. In pre-registered runs its lead over IPOP-CMA-ES holds under affine and box transformations, on box-constrained bbob and, pooled, on rotated f2-f4, but rotating the separable f3 and f4 removes most of the lead there. On held-out MA-BBOB its final share at 2000*D is level with IPOP-CMA-ES's and its anytime area lower. We published AMM as an open-source Python package (pip install amm-optimizer; https://github.com/BjBodner/amm-optimizer).

Multi-Step Performance Estimation for Gradient Descent under the Polyak Lojasiewicz Condition

Wanli Hong, Shuyang Ling

Gradient descent is a fundamental algorithm in machine learning and statistical inference. Performance estimation provides a systematic framework for obtaining sharp convergence bounds through semidefinite programming. We study the worst-case performance of constant-stepsize gradient descent over the class of $L$-smooth functions satisfying the $μ$-Polyak--Łojasiewicz (PŁ) inequality. We formulate the $N$-step objective-gap analysis as a semidefinite programming (SDP) relaxation based on pairwise smoothness constraints and pointwise PŁ inequalities. Since an exact interpolation condition for smooth PŁ functions is not currently available, the resulting SDP provides a valid upper bound on the true worst-case performance. We solve a reduced SDP retaining only consecutive-pair smoothness constraints analytically for every horizon $N$ and every stepsize $t\in(0,2/L)$. The solution exhibits three stepsize regimes. For $0<t\leq 1/L$ and $1/L<t\leq \sqrt{3}/L$, the reduced and full pairwise SDPs have the same optimal value, and the one-step SDP contraction factor tensorizes to a geometric $N$-step rate. For $\sqrt{3}/L<t<2/L$, the reduced-SDP value is nongeometric in the horizon: an initial transient phase is followed by eventual geometric decay. The resulting asymptotic contraction factor is tight for the full pairwise SDP and strictly improves the bound obtained by recursively applying the one-step estimate. Explicit primal and dual feasible solutions certify the results, with numerical SDP experiments guiding their construction. Our results show that analyzing several iterations together can give a better convergence bound than repeatedly applying the sharp one-step SDP bound in the Regime III.

Some algebraic and geometric computations in CH via Chow forms
