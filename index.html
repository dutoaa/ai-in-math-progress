At the Limits of Computability: Steps Towards Autonomous AI Discovery on the Busy Beaver Frontier

Marco Dos Santos, Vivek Veeriah, Alex Havrilla, Andrew Bolt, Goran Zuzic, Satinder Singh

Recent breakthroughs in AI for mathematics---ranging from Olympiad medal performances to resolving long-standing open conjectures---have rapidly expanded what automated systems can achieve. Yet these successes primarily concern predefined, human-formulated problems, leaving open how AI systems can autonomously uncover mathematical structure and formulate new questions. To push beyond static challenges, we explore the Busy Beaver (BB) domain, an uncomputable frontier that is fundamentally open-ended. Because no algorithm can compute the Busy Beaver number $\mathrm{BB}(n)$ for every $n$, this domain provides a natural setting for sustained exploration through mathematical conjecture and proof. In this paper, we introduce a Gemini-based agentic pipeline that automatically discovers accelerations of Turing machines, expressed as compact mathematical rules summarizing many execution steps. Our system integrates pattern recognition, automated conjecturing, informal mathematical reasoning, and formal theorem proving in Lean. With this framework, our system autonomously discovered and formally verified novel Collatz-like accelerations for eleven machines in $\mathrm{BB}(6)$. Subject to community acceptance, we provisionally classify these machines as Cryptids, namely machines whose blank-tape executions admit exact, compact mathematical descriptions but whose halting behavior remains unresolved. These results advance the analysis of $\mathrm{BB}(6)$ holdouts on the Busy Beaver frontier and demonstrate the potential of autonomous AI systems for open-ended mathematical discovery in fundamental computer science.

Corrected Block Sparse Spectral Optimization: Exact Models, Finite Identification, and Local Dynamics

Omid Shams Solari

The block generalized-power method pairs threshold-based support selection with inexpensive Stiefel updates, but the standard block formulation conflates two nonequivalent objectives and leaves inactive components, threshold ties, and post-selection refitting ill-specified. We rebuild the model from the primal. Eliminating each loading column from the normalized loading-ball problem gives a sum of soft-thresholded Euclidean norms (A-SUM); the squared-threshold objective in common use arises from a quadratic lift (A-SQ). We prove both identities, exhibit a two-component instance whose maximizers disagree, and specify explicit conventions for zero-response columns, ties, rank deficiency, and mask-before-refit. For A-SQ we separate the historical full-polar iteration from a safeguarded Riemannian ascent and prove, for the latter, whole-sequence convergence via a sufficient-ascent lemma, a relative-error lemma, and the Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz property, which applies because the soft-threshold objective and the Stiefel constraint are semi-algebraic. A strict threshold margin then forces finite identification of the support and sign pattern. The post-identification map is ordinary power iteration on the active covariance for a rank-one hard threshold, and the Frechet derivative of a coupled polar map for component-specific supports; a radius-based safe-screening rule follows from the same local analysis. Planted-spike experiments match the predicted identification time and local rates. On modern-AI panels (GPT-2 residual stream, ViT CLS stream, Pitprops) the thresholded block-sparse components match dense PCA on reconstruction and improve on it in monosemanticity and class specialisation. On three native-label scRNA datasets (Zheng17 FACS-sorted PBMCs, 68k donor-A with FACS-correlated labels, Tabula Muris) A-SQ beats dense PCA on cluster-recovery AMI by +0.023 to +0.044 at matched sparsity.

VibeTraining-Bench: Data-Trainer-Harness Co-Design for Iterative System Improvement

Ruotian Peng, Yuanyi Ren, Shuaiqi Liu, Xin Guo, Fang Zhu, Yangyi Huang

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) requires agents to diagnose system bottlenecks and coordinate improvements across training and inference. We refer to the agents' capability to jointly optimize training data, the trainer, and the inference harness as cross-component co-design. We introduce VibeTraining-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating this capability across tasks spanning terminal operation, software engineering, multi-turn tool use, and mathematical reasoning. Within each task, research agents start from an identical initial agent system under a fixed resource budget. Guided by validation feedback, they iteratively refine data, trainers, and inference harnesses, then select and submit a final agent system for evaluation on the test set. To prevent reward hacking, we enforce three safeguards: test data isolation, separate research and grading containers, and network restrictions for research agents. Our evaluation results show that frontier research agents substantially improve target agent systems. For example, on SWE-bench Verified, GPT-6-Astra raises the target agent system's performance from 32.9% to 53.7%, comparable to the human-developed instruct model's 55.4%. However, there is still room to improve their ability to co-design and iteratively refine data, trainer, and harness to achieve cumulative performance gains. We hope that VibeTraining-Bench will serve as a unified platform for advancing RSI, deepening our understanding of cross-component optimization for agent systems. Code and data are available at https://github.com/D2I-ai/VibeTrainingBench.

Maestro: Agentic Formal Proof Development by Coordination

Haoyu Li, Zeren Shui, Yuhao Zhang, Ferhat Erata, Benjamin Mikek, Murali Krishna Ramanathan

Lean is increasingly used to verify mathematical proofs and software produced by LLM agents, but writing formal proofs remains hard. Existing prover systems take a divide-and-conquer approach that recursively decomposes hard proof obligations into easier lemmas and splits again any lemma that resists proof. However, a lemma can resist proof for different reasons, including insufficient guidance, a false statement, or a flaw in the decomposition that produced it. Existing systems cannot tell these apart as a failed attempt reports little beyond its failure, and responses are confined to the failing node without considering the rest of the proof or revising the plan above it. The system may thus keep decomposing obligations along a flawed proof direction until the budget runs out, causing the whole proof attempt to fail. We introduce Maestro, a Lean prover harness that handles failures through global coordination. Provers report why they failed, not just that they failed, and a coordinator that sees the whole proof chooses an appropriate response, such as guiding a stuck prover, correcting a false lemma, or retracting a flawed subtree and replanning it. The same harness extends to verified code synthesis, where a checked counterexample sends the code back for repair. We evaluate Maestro with four frontier LLMs on seven mathematics and three software verification benchmarks. It proves more targets than each model's native coding agent (Claude Code or Codex) on every benchmark the agent has not already saturated, with the largest gains on research-level mathematics. With Claude Opus 5, it discovers its own proofs of 34 of the 48 theorems in the June 2026 release of arXivLean, against 19 for the native agent. It also outperforms all published prover systems on every benchmark they share, solving 669/672 PutnamBench, 59/60 Lean-IMO-Bench, and 6/6 IMO 2026.

Conformal Prediction-Enabled Sequence-level Policy Optimization for Reasoning Language Models

Hui Yang, Wan Tian, Tao Ren, Zhongyi Li, Yijie Peng

Reinforcement learning improves the reasoning capability of large language models through verifiable rewards for mathematical and coding tasks. In token-level methods such as GRPO, clipping acts on individual token ratios while the reward and advantage apply to a complete response. Recent sequence-level methods align these units but still use fixed clipping bands, whose tail probabilities change as the score distribution evolves. We propose Conformal Prediction-Enabled Sequence-level Policy Optimization (CSPO), which adaptively calibrates sequence-level clipping bounds using recent training statistics. The resulting band targets a specified marginal tail budget and adapts to policy drift; calibration conditional on response length requires additional assumptions. We bound the probability that a fresh score falls outside the band by the target miscoverage level plus explicit CDF discrepancy and drift terms. Under additional moment conditions, this bound also controls the objective and gradient error relative to the unclipped GSPO power surrogate. Experiments with the Qwen3-1.7B series show improvements over GSPO on difficult mathematical reasoning benchmarks and ALFWorld, with clipping endpoints that adapt during training.

Distributional Lifting for Discrete Optimization

Ningji Wei, Jiaming Liang

We introduce a framework that transforms discrete optimization into continuous optimization over parameterized probability distributions. The framework reveals a common distributional structure behind several methods that have largely developed independently, including continuous reformulations for multilinear binary optimization, Markov decision processes and reinforcement learning, the cross-entropy method, mean-field variational optimization, and the quantum approximate optimization algorithm. It enables flexible and modular algorithm design, for example, by using neural networks to incorporate contextual information or by enforcing convexity of the lifted problem. We establish two theoretical results: a link between the lifted optimality gap and sampling quality in the original discrete problem, and a convergence guarantee for the lifted optimization problem under suitable conditions. These results lead to a unified algorithmic template based on batched sampling and stochastic gradient updates that is naturally compatible with GPU computation. We demonstrate the framework through three case studies spanning multidimensional knapsack, covering-constrained three-dimensional assignment, and a stochastic mixed-integer location-allocation problem, showing that it can accommodate distinct combinatorial structures and scale to large discrete and mixed-integer settings.

Newton-Based Methods for Bilevel Optimization Beyond Lower-Level Strong Convexity

Youran Dong, Junfeng Yang, Wei Yao, Jin Zhang

Bilevel optimization provides a framework for machine learning tasks such as hyperparameter optimization, data reweighting, and meta-learning. Existing Newton-based bilevel methods typically rely on the classical hypergradient formula, which is generally unavailable when lower-level solutions are nonunique and the lower-level Hessian is singular. We develop two Newton-based methods beyond lower-level strong convexity by exploiting curvature shared by the lower-level and multiplier updates. Newton-based GALET handles potentially singular lower-level curvature under a positive spectral-gap condition, exploiting its range structure to avoid the squared-Hessian multiplier system used in GALET. Newton-based BAMM uses a strongly convex aggregation model when the lower-level objective is convex and the upper-level objective is strongly convex in the lower-level variable. We establish convergence guarantees and improved complexity bounds relative to GALET and sl-BAMM under the corresponding assumptions. We further introduce GNBO, a generalized Newton-based framework that captures the shared-curvature structure of the proposed methods and several existing Newton and quasi-Newton bilevel methods. Experiments on synthetic problems, data hyper-cleaning, and GPT-2 data reweighting evaluate the proposed methods across different bilevel learning tasks.

IF-MAP: Influence-Function-Inspired Agent Prioritization in Multi-Agent Systems

Jie Mao, Pingchen Lu, Zikun Qu, Jiahang Sun, Xiangyi Wang, Zhiwei Shang

Improving every agent in an LLM-based multi-agent system can be prohibitively costly, making it important to identify which agent should be prioritized to achieve the greatest system-level gain with limited resources. Failure attribution can inform this decision, but an agent's responsibility for failures does not directly reveal the system-level gains achievable by improving it. We therefore introduce IF-MAP, an influence-function-inspired method for identifying agents with high potential for system-level improvement. For each agent, IF-MAP measures end-to-end loss changes under controlled semantic prompt perturbations, fits a regularized local response model in a low-dimensional prompt-embedding subspace, and uses the estimated response norm as a priority score. This black-box procedure requires no access to model parameters or gradients and produces agent priorities that can guide subsequent improvement effort. We evaluate IF-MAP across six multi-agent workflows spanning code generation, question answering, and mathematical reasoning, using the same priorities for prompt optimization and model upgrades. Optimizing the selected agents' prompts achieves a macro-average gain of 3.81 percentage points, compared with 1.85 for uniform agent selection. Across five target models, upgrading the selected agents yields macro-average gains of 7.62-16.42 percentage points, compared with 3.81-8.62 for uniform agent selection. Despite its probing overhead, IF-MAP reduces Qwen3-8B and DeepSeek-V3.2 token use by 74.2% and 35.2%, respectively, relative to optimizing every agent, and reduces target-model tokens by 63.6-64.9% relative to upgrading every agent. These results support the use of IF-MAP to identify promising agents and direct improvement effort toward greater end-to-end performance gains.

A Deep Backward Scheme for Reflected BSDEs via Penalization : Approximation and Convergence Analysis

E. H. Essaky, M. A. Jalal, Y. Ouknine

We propose and analyze a deep backward numerical scheme for Markovian reflected backward stochastic differential equations with a generator independent of the control variable, based on the penalization method. The reflection constraint is replaced by penalized BSDEs, which are approximated backward in time by neural networks, with particular attention to the dependence of the errors on the penalization parameter \(λ\). We derive quantitative error estimates for both the value process \(Y\) and the control process \(Z\). A key ingredient is an \(L^2\)-regularity estimate for the control component with explicit dependence on \(λ\). Exploiting the monotonicity of the penalization term, we obtain stability estimates uniform in \(λ\) and prove uniform spatial Lipschitz bounds for both the penalized value functions and the discrete backward targets. Consequently, the GroupSort Lipschitz constraints and the associated neural-network approximation error can be controlled uniformly in \(λ\). The global analysis separates penalization, time-discretization, forward, and neural-network approximation errors. Balancing the dominant terms yields a mean-square error of order \(O(|π|^{1/3})\) for the value component, together with a corresponding estimate for a post-training reconstruction of \(Z\). Numerical experiments with an explicit reflected BSDE and American option problems illustrate the scheme on one-dimensional and structured high-dimensional benchmarks.

Deep Pontryagin Learning for Continuous-Time Stochastic Control with Jumps

Jean-Loup Dupret, Patrick Cheridito

We develop a deep Pontryagin learning framework for high-dimensional continuous-time stochastic control problems that naturally accommodates jump-diffusions. In contrast to direct policy optimization, which repeatedly differentiates the objective through the entire time-discretized dynamics, our method separates adjoint learning from policy improvement by explicitly incorporating state-sensitivity information from the Pontryagin stochastic maximum principle. For a fixed Markov control, we first compute pathwise adjoint labels by backpropagating the reward through time along simulated state trajectories, and project these labels onto the Markov state through a supervised regression step. The control is then updated by enforcing the pointwise Hamiltonian stationarity condition. We prove that the resulting projected adjoint process satisfies a discrete-time Pontryagin principle and converges, as the time step tends to zero, to the solution of the corresponding continuous-time adjoint backward stochastic differential equation. Numerical experiments illustrate the accuracy, stability, and scalability of the approach.

GLOBE: Geometry-Led Online Behavioral Early Exit via Unit-Sphere Trajectories

Xinyue Peng, Zhenlin Liang, Meng Cai, Hua Wang

Chain-of-thought (CoT) reasoning can improve problem solving, but models often keep generating after the correct answer is already reachable; the extra steps raise serving costs and may replace a correct intermediate result with an incorrect one. We propose GLOBE (Geometry-Led Online Behavioral Early Exit), an online, training-free algorithm that terminates the model's CoT process early. GLOBE embeds each completed reasoning segment as a point on a unit sphere and reads the shape of the resulting semantic trajectory. GLOBE first applies a trial phase that generates short trial answers in a separate branch, stopping the main stream on an accepted trial after checking confidence against a threshold. If generation continues, a trajectory phase stops the stream once this path repeatedly reverses direction with little net progress. On five benchmarks including mathematical reasoning, tool calling, and agent memory, GLOBE cuts output tokens by 49.7% and active end-to-end wall time (E2E) by 51.0% across two models, and achieves a five-task average of 80.58%, comparable to Full CoT.

On the possibilities and limitations of LLM-based multi-agent systems for multi-criteria decision-making in healthcare operations management

Multi-criteria models for healthcare operations need weights that express how stakeholders trade off patient waiting time, provider workload, and operating cost, but these weights are difficult to quantify. Teams of large language model (LLM) agents, each prompted to represent a stakeholder, have been used to supply them. We propose a framework for assessing the reliability of LLM-generated preferences as inputs to multi-objective optimization and quantifying how their uncertainty affects decisions. We study explicit preferences by asking agents to rate the importance of the criteria in an outpatient appointment scheduling problem, and implicit preferences by inferring trade-offs from the agents' pairwise choices between emergency department configurations. The computational experiments show that the explicit preferences respond to the clinic's operating conditions and differ across roles, but deliberation among the agents can erase these differences, whereas simple averaging preserves them. The pairwise choices used to reveal the agents' implicit preferences depend largely on which option is presented first, and we find limited evidence that a statistical model using contextual information alongside presentation order predicts the agents' choices on new comparison pairs better than a model using presentation order alone. We then formulate robust weighted-sum optimization models to account for preference uncertainty. In most cases, these models select different schedules or configurations from those obtained using average preference weights. These results indicate that LLM-generated preferences need an audit of this kind before they are used as optimization weights in healthcare decision support.
