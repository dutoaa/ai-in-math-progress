Atomic MathReasoner: Instance-Level Composition of Atomic Reasoning Skills for Mathematical LLMs

Jiashu Li, Tianyu Gao, Qinglong Liu, Jinping Wang, Ye Wei, Zhiqiang Gao

Most existing approaches enhance mathematical reasoning in large language models (LLMs) as a monolithic capability, despite the fact that different mathematical problems require different reasoning skills. Recent work has explored a more fine-grained perspective by decomposing mathematical reasoning into atomic skills and developing specialized modules for individual capabilities. However, how to effectively utilize these atomic abilities remains underexplored. To address this challenge, we propose Atomic MathReasoner (AMR), an instance-level framework for composing atomic reasoning skills. AMR first trains a library of capability-specific LoRA experts and performs gradient-free optimization over held-out validation subsets to discover a diverse set of low-loss composition prototypes. It evaluates the preference of each prototype using the token-level negative log-likelihood of reference solutions, filters ambiguous assignments through a confidence margin, and distills the resulting pseudo-labels into a lightweight router. During inference, the router selects an appropriate composition prototype for each problem, enabling problem-specific utilization of atomic skills without costly online optimization. Experiments on the AtomicMath benchmark across general and mathematics-specialized LLMs demonstrate consistent improvements. On Qwen2.5-Math-Instruct-7B, AMR increases average field accuracy from 64.4% to 68.1% at Level 1 and from 59.3% to 63.7% at Level 2, while improving average logical reasoning performance from 39.4% to 46.8%. These results demonstrate that effectively composing specialized atomic skills provides a promising approach to strengthening mathematical reasoning in LLMs.

The Four-Element Theory of Structure-Driven Methodology: A Methodological Shift from the Perspective of Natural Science and Engineering Practice

Shengchang Chen

Mainstream artificial intelligence systems, fundamentally driven by the Optimization-Driven (OD) paradigm, rely on setting external objectives and iterative trial-and-error. When confronted with complex nonlinear problems containing noise and conflicting constraints, this paradigm inherently induces statistical smoothing and compromise, yielding black-box models that lack interpretability and out-of-distribution generalization. To address these limitations, this paper proposes the Four-Element Theory of Structure-Driven Methodology, reconstructing the solving process into four irreversible stages: Deconstruction, Structure, Construction, and Solving. The method suspends a priori presuppositions to strip unit coupling, extracts rigid invariants inherent to the problem, constructs equivalent mappings anchored to these invariants, and executes the mapping directly to generate the solution. Evaluation metrics and experience are relegated to a posteriori verification. Demonstrated through cross-disciplinary practices including number theory, Hamiltonian systems, deep learning, and seismic inversion, this methodology transcends the statistical fitting bottleneck. Structure-Driven and Objective-Driven methodologies are complementary: when structure is sufficient, solutions are directly constructed; when lacking, it degenerates into the OD path.

The FrontierOR Challenge: Agentic Algorithm Design for Operations Research

Hai Wang, Bretislav Hajek, Chonghe Jiang, Minwei Kong, Ao Qu, Huatian Gong

Operations research (OR) relies on algorithms that experts tailor to each problem: a new routing, scheduling or network design problem can take weeks of specialist work before it is solved well at scale. Large language model (LLM) agents now write, test and improve code, but whether they can design algorithms that scale to realistic instances is open: in the FrontierOR benchmark's one-shot evaluation, the strongest model matches Gurobi, with an objective within 1% of Gurobi's and no slower, on only 39% of the large instances. The FrontierOR Challenge asks teams to build agentic workflows that design, test, and improve algorithms for many optimization problems at once. Stage 1 releases 60 of the benchmark's 180 audited problems, spanning six application categories and five formulation types, in weekly rounds of 15; Stage 2 introduces new problems outside the benchmark, with unseen instances. Two tracks are ranked separately: in the Main track we run the submitted algorithms in a network-isolated sandbox with open-source dependencies only; in the Self-hosted track teams solve the instances on their own machines, with any software, within a timed window. Every solution is checked for feasibility, and a feasible solution scores the ratio of its objective to a frozen known reference, averaged over hidden instances.

A Systematic Analysis of a Small Optimal Subspace for Physics-Informed Neural Networks: $n$-DAS $\subset \mathbb{R}^p$

Ahmad AliAhmad, Khemraj Shukla

The search for a low-dimensional subspace that governs neural network training has been a persistent goal in optimization. Yet subspaces defined by Hessian eigenvalue magnitude, such as the dominant/bulk decomposition underlying Dom-SGD and Bulk-SGD, have repeatedly failed to capture where optimization takes place, and adaptive Krylov-based alternatives built on energy-fraction thresholds show promise but still struggle at lower eigenmodes. We argue this failure is structural: the directions of highest curvature are not, in general, the directions along which training moves. We introduce the dominant aligned subspace (DAS), constructed from a full eigendecomposition of the Hessian but selected by gradient alignment rather than eigenvalue magnitude, as a tool for understanding optimization dynamics and a foundation for designing methods that exploit them. To expose this structure under the hardest conditions, we use physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) as a testbed: differential operators from the parabolic, elliptic, and hyperbolic classes induce severely ill-conditioned, anisotropic loss landscapes. Across Poisson, heat, wave, and viscous Burgers benchmarks DAS-restricted training with a line search matches full-space training even where the projected Hessian remains ill-conditioned, showing empirically that convergence does not require all Hessian eigendirections but only a low-dimensional, gradient-aligned subset. We extend the analysis to Gauss-Newton within the DAS and discuss what this structure implies for future subspace-aware optimizers.

PWLNet: A Framework for Linearized Neural Surrogates in Nonlinear Process Optimization

Sunoy Sanyal, Canberk Demir, Metin Türkay, Erdal Aydın

Neural networks represent nonlinear process systems accurately, but embedding them in optimization is difficult because smooth activations introduce nonlinear, nonconvex constraints. PWLNet is a Python framework for building optimization-compatible neural surrogates through exact and piecewise-linear reformulations. It combines baseline and physics-informed training, optimization-aware pruning, and Pyomo embeddings based on ReLU mixed-integer linear models, exact tanh nonlinear models, PWL-tanh formulations, and binary-free SOSX branch-and-LP. PWL-native training uses the same activation in training and embedding, avoiding extra linearization error. It is evaluated on Boston housing, the Williams-Otto process, the Van de Vusse reactor, and a hydroformylation semi-batch reactor. On Boston housing, SOSX training gave the best common-architecture model (test MAE 1.984, R2 = 0.925). On Williams-Otto, pruning 15% of hidden neurons reduced MILP solution time 6.5-fold while preserving the selected operating point. For the Van de Vusse reactor, a physics-informed ReLU RNN pruned from 32 to 12 hidden units enabled tractable exact MILP embedding and a 4% true-plant economic gain over the best ramp-and-hold policy, while terminal-aware shrinking-horizon prediction reached 99.7% of the direct-shooting reference. For the hydroformylation reactor, one-step exact-tanh IPOPT reached 99.2% of the certified reference product with peak pressure 20.002 bar (limit 20 bar) and 14.36 s total solve time over 280 moves; ReLU and PWL-native controllers achieved comparable recovery, with ReLU fastest. Longer horizons and physics-informed training did not consistently improve closed-loop results. Overall, compact linearized neural surrogates enable accurate, tractable optimization in steady-state and dynamic applications, with constraint fidelity identified as a key direction for improvement.

When and why can integral constraints improve the accuracy of physics-informed neural networks?

Myeong-Su Lee

Adding constraints obtained by integrating governing equations to the loss has been reported to improve the accuracy of physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) in several applications, but mathematical understanding of these benefits remains limited. For several partial differential equations (PDEs), we derive stability estimates for losses with and without integral constraints. The comparison reveals that in certain parameter regimes, the standard loss has very weak control over specific error components, and that the integral constraints restore uniform control of these components. This explains when and why the constraints improve accuracy. Finally, numerical experiments support this interpretation. In parameter regimes where the standard loss is predicted to have weak control over those components, the two losses indeed show clear differences in accuracy. Conversely, in regimes where the standard loss retains sufficient control, both losses yield similar accuracy.

Stability of Parallel Transport Convolution under Riemannian Metric Perturbations

Hongnian Huang, Andrew Stump

Geometric convolutions on Riemannian manifolds depend on the geometry of the underlying domain, making stability under metric perturbations a fundamental question in geometric deep learning. This paper studies this question for parallel transport convolution through methods from differential geometry and analysis. On a fixed compact manifold, we establish that, for a continuous input signal and a continuous reference kernel supported in a sufficiently small normal neighborhood, the convolution output depends continuously in $L^2$ on the Riemannian metric in the $C^2$-topology, under the specified localization and transport-path conventions. The analysis develops perturbation estimates for geodesics, parallel transport, Jacobi fields, and exponential maps, and uses these estimates to compare the transported kernels. A central difficulty is the cut locus, which obstructs uniform comparison of the transport construction over the entire manifold. We address this difficulty by combining uniform estimates away from the cut locus with integral bounds on neighborhoods of arbitrarily small volume, while also controlling the change in the Riemannian volume measure. The resulting qualitative stability theorem connects perturbation theory for Riemannian geometric constructions with the analysis of spatial convolution operators in geometric deep learning.

Stochastic counterpart of Nesterov's method for sampling and provable non-acceleration of samplers with heavy-ball structure

A. Sharma

Polyak's heavy-ball method and Nesterov's method are two momentum-based methods that have inspired many optimization techniques used in applied fields, including machine learning. Momentum also provides a connection with sampling, and interestingly, many kinetic Langevin samplers reduce to the heavy-ball recurrence when velocity is eliminated and noise is removed. This observation motivates a natural question: what is the corresponding sampler for Nesterov's method? We propose a sampler whose deterministic part is exactly Nesterov's recurrence, driven by temporally correlated Gaussian noise and requires one new gradient evaluation per step. Noise correlation provides $\mathcal{O}(h^2)$ bias for smooth observables. We establish geometric ergodicity for possibly non-convex potentials. For strongly convex potentials with Lipschitz continuous gradients, we obtain contraction rates and show that, up to an additive constant, the energy decays at a rate of order $κ^{-1/2}$, where $κ$ is the condition number. For Gaussian targets, we show that relaxation has square-root dependence on $κ$. We further prove that any sampler based on heavy-ball dynamics with additive noise cannot achieve accelerated convergence uniformly over the class of strongly convex functions. Finally, we compare the proposed sampler with OBABO, BAOAB, HMC, MALA, BPS, and NUTS in numerical experiments and demonstrate the better performance of proposed sampler.

Differentially Private Score Estimation with Applications to Score-Based Generative Models

However, learning scores from sensitive data raises privacy concerns. These concerns are particularly pronounced in score-based generative modeling, where trained models may memorize individual training examples and expose sensitive information through membership inference and data extraction attacks. Although non-private score estimation has been extensively studied, the statistical limits of its private counterpart remain less understood. In this work, we study score estimation under $(α,β)$-differential privacy for $d$-dimensional sub-Gaussian distributions with locally $γ$-Hölder-continuous scores. We also establish a nearly matching private minimax lower bound, thereby characterizing the privacy-accuracy trade-off for score estimation. As an application, we construct a differentially private diffusion model whose privacy guarantee is independent of the number of denoising steps. We show that, with an appropriate discretization scheme, the resulting diffusion model attains nearly minimax optimal expected total variation error among all private distribution estimators. We validate our theoretical findings through experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets.

Infinity-Wasserstein Distributionally Robust Principal Component Analysis: Exact Reformulation, Finite-Sample Guarantee, and Efficient Algorithm

This paper demonstrates how the infinity-Wasserstein distance can reconcile these two desirable properties. For the resulting DRO model of PCA, we derive an exact finite-dimensional reformulation and explicitly identify a worst-case distribution attaining the inner maximum. Crucially, the reformulated objective retains samplewise residual information beyond the empirical covariance matrix, revealing a mechanism through which distributional robustness can reshape the learned principal subspace. Under the mild assumption that the true distribution has a finite fourth moment, we further establish a uniform finite-sample performance guarantee for the proposed model. An iteratively reweighted majorization-minimization algorithm is also developed to solve the resulting nonconvex and nonsmooth optimization problem, with a rigorous analysis of its convergence properties. Numerical experiments substantiate the computational efficiency of the proposed algorithm and the practical effectiveness of the infinity-Wasserstein model in downstream applications.
