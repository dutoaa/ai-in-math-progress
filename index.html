What the Machine Invented: Mining New Mathematical Instruments from OpenAI's October 2026 Release of AI-Generated Mathematics

Ying Xie

On 6 October 2026 OpenAI published an initial 722 AI-written mathematical manuscripts, organized into 372 problem families, in the public repository github.com/openai/math. The release includes claimed solutions to many prominent open problems. We approach this collection as a treasure mine of mathematical ideas. Our aim is to extract new lemmas, constructions, inequalities and proof mechanisms introduced in the source arguments, and to distinguish them from established tools and new combinations of those tools. We call these mathematical instruments. Extraction comes first; their validity, novelty and possible reuse then become subjects for further study. The resulting descriptions also help readers understand the selected papers without having to reconstruct every argument at the outset. We examine 38 families in two groups of 17 and 21, spanning number theory, geometry, analysis, combinatorics, complexity, algebra, operator algebras, topology and mathematical physics. Each entry records the claim, extracted instruments, antecedents and caveats. Eleven cross-cutting patterns connect the entries, and a scoreboard records formalization status and dependencies. Novelty assessments are provisional and sometimes mixed. Selected source comparisons correct mathematical statements, hypotheses and counts; they do not independently establish priority or certify the underlying proofs. No Lean development was compiled or kernel-checked for this survey. We also briefly describe a subsequent application of the signed restriction lemma from family 112 to circuit lower bounds at every fixed depth.

Boundary Control of a Modified Timoshenko-Ehrenfest Beam via Reduced-Order Reinforcement Learning

Leonardo R S Rodrigues

In this paper, we study the active vibration control of an elastic beam using reinforcement learning (RL). The physical system is modeled by a modified Timoshenko-Ehrenfest type linear partial differential equation with simply supported boundary conditions. The dynamics are approximated by a reduced-order model (ROM) obtained through eigenvalue expansion and truncated Galerkin projection. Control is applied through boundary actuation. The resulting finite-dimensional system is formulated as a continuous-time Markov decision process and solved numerically using the Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) algorithm. All modeling steps, from physical formulation to temporal discretization and control design, are explicitly described. A systematic comparison is performed between the controller learned via PPO and the classical linear quadratic (LQR) regulator. The analysis highlights the strengths and limitations of each approach, both from a theoretical and practical point of view, with particular emphasis on energy dissipation mechanisms and actuator constraints.

TPPO: Tracking-Value PPO For Language Model Reasoning

Futing Wang, Chenxi Li, Jianhao Yan, Yun Luo, Zhi Wang, Yizhuo Li

Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) is widely used in reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards for language-model reasoning. While \emph{value evolution along trajectory} characterizes relative token-level credit, yet standard critics often fail to track it under sparse terminal rewards. To model this evolution, we analyze the relation between successive prefix values and show that they admit a Bayesian decomposition with additive updates in log-odds space. We use this additive structure to construct Tracking-Value PPO (\method), a cumulative value parameterization that sums position-wise scalar outputs into value logits. These outputs are learned through terminal-outcome supervision on the reconstructed values. \method improves aggregate mathematical reasoning scores over PPO by 7.0 and 8.4 points on Qwen3-4B-Base and Qwen3-8B-Base, respectively, while improving out-of-distribution generalization. Gains extend to Qwen3-4B, Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen3.5-9B and to countdown, and scientific coding tasks. Further analysis shows more accurate tracking of value evolution and more reliable advantage estimates. Our code is publicly available in https://github.com/LINs-lab/TPPO.

Growing and Transferring Pathways for LLM Reinforcement Learning

Haolin Tian, Yikang Li, Tonghan Wang

Reinforcement learning (RL) can substantially improve the reasoning capabilities of large language models, but an effective training run requires considerable resources. Reusing the computation acquired through RL across models would extend the value of that training. We propose GrowRL, an RL method that organizes learned updates into compact computational pathways through structural growth. GrowRL freezes the pretrained backbone and adds an independent causal attention branch and a feed-forward network branch at each Transformer layer. Zero-initialized output projections preserve the pretrained policy at initialization, and RL updates only the added pathways. The learned pathways remain explicit after training, allowing transfer to structurally compatible target models and subsequent adaptation with the target backbone frozen. The architecture also supports sequential transfer of additional pathways. By keeping the learned attention and feed-forward computation separate from the pretrained weights, GrowRL provides an explicit structure for preserving and reusing the outcome of an RL run. Experiments across five backbones show competitive mathematical reasoning and out-of-distribution performance with a small number of trainable parameters. Learned pathways provide immediate gains across all three transfer directions, with performance after only 10 target-side updates already close to that after 100 updates in every case. These results support computational pathways as parameter-efficient and reusable units of reinforcement learning adaptation.

Exact Topological Compliance: Generating Persistence-Equivalent Graphs

Mattie Ji, Indradyumna Roy, Vikas Garg

Persistent homology summarizes the topological evolution of a graph as persistence diagrams (PDs). Generating graphs whose topology matches a target descriptor is increasingly important, yet existing topology-aware generative models rely on soft regularization, producing graphs whose PDs may only be approximately similar. We initiate a principled investigation of the generation problem: produce graphs that realize a target PD exactly. We propose two approaches guided by the implicit local and global constraints a target PD imposes. The local approach builds degree-based PD-equivalent graphs iteratively, and we prove that every output realizes the target PD and that every degree-based PD-equivalent graph is reachable. However, as shown thereafter, local generation necessarily trades off between sample diversity and reaching infeasible states. We therefore recast generation as a global constraint satisfaction problem with a complete encoding solvable by modern constraint programming solvers. This is applicable to any vertex-based permutation equivariant filtration scheme and is minimal under the degree filtration. Experiments on four standard graph generation benchmarks confirm that both methods realize the target PD exactly. Together, our methods provide a principled treatment of strict topology-compliant graph generation. Code is available at https://github.com/Aalto-QuML/ExactCompliance.

LeanPRM: Lean-Grounded Process Supervision for Natural Language Proof Verification

Rodney Lafuente-Mercado, Salman Rahman, Jingyan Shen, James Shiffer, Saadia Gabriel, Pavel Izmailov

AI systems now write long and difficult mathematical proofs, and checking their step-by-step validity is becoming a bottleneck. A learned verifier could check proofs cheaply, but training one requires a correctness label on every step, and such labels are hard to obtain for open-ended proofs. Existing methods take them from human annotators, which is costly, or derive them from final answers, which proofs do not have, and formalizing each proof in Lean requires expert effort. We introduce LeanPRM, a framework that uses the Lean proof assistant to create step-level labels for natural-language proofs. A model writes a natural-language proof together with a paired formal proof, without feedback from Lean. Lean then labels every step, and a second model discards failures caused only by the formal syntax, so that the negative labels mark errors in the mathematics. LeanPRM contains 10,634 proofs with 152K labeled steps and no human annotation. A Qwen3-4B verifier trained on LeanPRM raises exact step-label accuracy on a held-out set of 200 proofs (LeanPRM-Bench) from 48.2 to 74.7, as opposed to 60.3 when the same base model is trained on PRM800K. It also selects better proofs in best-of-N selection on IMO-level problems, and outperforms the PRM800K-trained verifier on three of four external verification benchmarks. Training on LeanPRM improves verifiers at every size from 0.6B to 8B parameters on LeanPRM-Bench, Hard2Verify and IMO-GradingBench. Given the labels need no human annotation, LeanPRM can grow with the supply of formal statements, offering a path to step-level feedback on AI-generated proofs that are hard for humans to check.

Neural networks (NNs) have been employed in various ways to accelerate the solution of multi-parametric optimization problems (OPs), which prominently arise in model predictive control (MPC). Early approaches focused on approximating the parametric solution (e.g., the optimal control law) directly by NNs. More recently, learning-based techniques have been used to emulate or enhance solver iterations. For the direct approximation of the parametric solution, it has been demonstrated that exploiting structural properties of the underlying OP facilitates the design of suitable NN architectures. In this paper, we show that structural analysis can also be leveraged to systematically integrate learning into classical optimization algorithms, thereby enhancing their performance. We formalize our concept for the case of multi-parametric quadratic programs (mpQPs), which frequently arise in linear-quadratic MPC, as a representative application. More precisely, we demonstrate that tailored NNs enable a parameter-dependent fine-tuning of the step sizes in a standard dual projected gradient scheme. The design of the NNs is guided by the observation that optimal step sizes, which permit obtaining the exact optimal solution in finitely many iterations, are piecewise constant functions over the parameter space (e.g., the state space in MPC). We establish key theoretical properties of the proposed approach and illustrate its effectiveness in a numerical case study.

Output Discipline Sets the Floor: A Falsification-Guided, Model-Agnostic Agent Protocol for Auditable Hypothesis-Driven Decision Making

Jing Liu, Yongxing Qi, Ping Yao, Ting Wu

What binds large language models on auditable, hypothesis-driven decision tasks, where every candidate can be checked against the evidence? On the public TCMEval-SDT benchmark for Traditional Chinese Medicine syndrome differentiation (200 cases), the binding constraint is output discipline and reasoning structure, not model capability alone: two years of model scaling move zero-shot scores by only -0.03 to +0.08, while output-discipline failures (non-terminating reasoning, format non-compliance, large run-to-run spread) persist even in frontier models. We test this with a model-agnostic agent framework formalizing decision making as a five-stage closed loop, enforced by a candidate x evidence matrix falsification protocol: every candidate is judged against every evidence item, at least half of the candidates must be falsified before a conclusion is licensed, and survivors are emitted in a machine-verifiable single-line format. Across five base models, the effect is sign-conditional on zero-shot output discipline: it rescues models whose discipline fails (DeepSeek-V4-Flash: +0.267/+0.271, entirely termination rescue), reduces a high-variance model's run-to-run standard deviation from 0.218 to 0.007 (Kimi K3), and significantly costs already-disciplined models (-0.087 to -0.120 on budget-matched Qwen3-4B/32B). Ablations attribute the cost to the candidate-bounded format layer and show the effect is independent of injected knowledge content (protocol-knowledge decoupling). The protocol also transfers to misinformation-robust mathematical reasoning, detecting injected false premises at 89.8% recall with a 6.8% false-positive rate (MCC 0.54; a flag-everything baseline scores 0) and restoring accuracy to the no-misinformation ceiling. Where zero-shot output discipline is weak, the reasoning protocol sets the floor; base-model capability always sets the ceiling.

Experiments using Qwen3-14B as the teacher and Dream-7B as the student demonstrate that JustOPD consistently improves mathematical reasoning performance over the base DLM across multiple divergence choices.

K. Rabiei, Z. Rui, X. Lu, W. Hao

Finite-horizon optimal control becomes difficult when a dynamical model can be simulated reliably, but its derivatives are unavailable, discontinuous, or unreliable. Hamiltonian Primal-Dual Policy Optimization (HPDPO) is introduced as a derivative-free, model-based reinforcement learning framework for this setting. The control is represented as a time-indexed finite-horizon policy. Candidate actions are evaluated by deterministic suffix rollouts of the supplied transition model, while a regularized Hamiltonian dual formulation supplies structured future-cost information through block-coordinate multiplier updates. No differentiation of the state dynamics or learned critic is required. The method is validated on nonsmooth problems with exact solutions, an analytical and learned ReLU biomarker cascade, an amyloid Neural ODE trained from longitudinal ADNI data, and three multidimensional-control problems with up to five simultaneous controls. Across these examples, HPDPO closely reproduces exact or reference objective values, trajectories, and control structures. The results show that forward simulation and Hamiltonian dual structure can be combined to solve deterministic finite-horizon control problems involving nonsmooth, learned, or black-box dynamics.

Diffusion large language models (dLLMs) have attracted increasing research attention due to their bidirectional attention and arbitrary order decoding. However, there are two issues in dLLMs inference: the decoding difficulty is dominated by the tokens on the right side of the block, and the block-wise decoding inherently suffers from boundary contextual discontinuity. To tackle these limitations, we propose HaloBlock, a difficulty-aware adaptive halo mechanism for dLLM inference. We first leverage bidirectional cumulative entropy to characterize block decoding difficulty. Then, based on the bidirectional decoding difficulty, we adaptively allocate bidirectional halo regions on both sides of each block to mitigate boundary contextual discontinuity. The difficulty-aware design of HaloBlock smooths cross-block context propagation and lowers decoding difficulty for hard tokens, yielding better generation quality and faster convergence. Evaluated on LLaDA-8B-Instruct, Dream-7B-Instruct and LLaDA-1.5 across mathematical reasoning and code generation benchmarks, HaloBlock demonstrates consistent accuracy improvements and higher inference efficiency. Source codes are publicly available at https://github.com/PolyU-VCLab/HaloBlock.

An efficient shallow Ritz method was developed in [8, 9] for one-dimensional linear diffusion and diffusion-reaction problems. In this paper, the method is extended to a nonlinear diffusion-reaction equation with a cubic reaction term: (1) a basic error estimate of the shallow Ritz discretization is established and (2) a reduced block Newton (rBN) method for solving the resulting non-convex optimization problem is developed. Again, the computational cost of the rBN method is $\mathcal{O}(n)$ for one-dimensional problems, where $n$ is the number of neurons. Combining a convex splitting scheme for time discretization with the efficient shallow Ritz method, we develop a time-stepping neural network method for solving the Allen-Cahn equation. The unconditional energy stability of the time discretization and an error estimate in the $L^2$ norm are established. Numerical experiments demonstrate that the neural network approach yields accurate solutions for singularly perturbed nonlinear diffusion-reaction equations and the Allen-Cahn equation. The method effectively resolves interior and boundary layers without oscillations and significantly outperforms the finite element method on fixed uniform meshes.

We propose a new model-agnostic loss function approach to estimate optimal order quantities using observational data, and characterize the optimal newsvendor policy as the policy that minimizes our proposed loss function. Our method requires only a single estimation step, can be scaled to datasets with hundreds of thousands of data points, and does not suffer from the same instabilities as inverse censoring probability weighting methods. Moreover, we show through numerical experiments that our method quickly converges to the optimal policy on synthetic data, and leads to improved performance on real world retail data.

Beyond Policy Rollouts: Embedding-Space Directional Exploration with Anchored Noise for LLM RLVR

Daijiao Liu, Huiyi Wang, Lina Yao, Dong Gong

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) typically uses small rollout groups per prompt, where repeated sampling from the same policy can yield highly similar trajectories, reducing within-group reward variation and weakening group-relative advantage signals. Existing remedies such as higher sampling temperature, entropy bonuses, asymmetric clipping, and larger rollout budgets broaden sampling or modify policy updates but leave rollout conditioning unchanged. In this paper, we propose EDEN (Embedding-space Directional Exploration via anchored Noise), which intervenes at the rollout source through anchored input perturbations. EDEN explores beyond the policy's own rollouts. Within the same rollout budget, it replaces a small fraction of standard rollouts in each group with rollouts that the same policy generates from the prompt plus a single appended, perturbed embedding position, rather than from the prompt alone. Gaussian noise drives variation, while a shared anchor provides a common conditioning direction within each prompt group. Selected perturbed rollouts contribute to GRPO updates with downweighted advantages, and the model architecture, discrete decoding, and test-time inference remain unchanged. Across model scales and mathematical reasoning benchmarks, EDEN outperforms GRPO and the exploration controls evaluated in the same pipeline, with the largest gains in sampled solution coverage. Analyses show that more prompt groups provide non-zero group-relative learning signals and that noise and anchoring play complementary roles. Intervening at the rollout source is thus an effective lever for exploration in RLVR.

VeriPIE: Checkable Query Plans and Evidence-Guided Selection for Text-to-SQL

Financial Artificial Intelligence (AI) governance and model risk management (MRM) emphasize independent validation, transparency, and effective challenge of AI-generated computations. Text-to-SQL translates natural-language questions into Structured Query Language (SQL), but queries that agree on one database can still express different computations. We present VeriPIE, a neurosymbolic framework combining language-model generation with symbolic checks. It ensembles direct SQL generation with QuerySketch, a structured plan language checked by a backend implemented in Lean, a theorem prover and programming language. Plan diagnostics guide repair before SQL generation, while execution agreement and bounded searches for distinguishing databases inform candidate selection. These checks expose malformed plans and hidden disagreement without proving agreement with the user's intent. In single-run experiments on the complete Spider development set, VeriPIE achieves 72.05% test-suite accuracy (745/1,034), compared with DeepEye's 65.67% (679/1,034). Its two-query shortlist covers 78.43% (811 questions), compared with 74.66% for DIN-SQL (772) and 69.05% for DeepEye (714). On BIRD, VeriPIE reaches 68.90% execution accuracy and 73.73% two-query coverage. The central benefit is reviewable evidence about the generated computation: explicit plan checks, repair diagnostics, and concrete disagreements that support safer financial analysis.
