Auditing AI peer reviewers: a benchmark on real scientific papers

Íñigo Zubeldia, Boris Bolliet, Francisco Villaescusa-Navarro, Pablo Villanueva-Domingo, Miles Cranmer

LLM-based peer review is now used at production scale, but its evaluation remains limited. We introduce a benchmark that injects objective, localisable errors of four types (general issues, mathematics, numerics, citations) at three severity levels into real papers and measures each reviewer's detection rate by error type and severity, together with its false-positive rate on clean copies. We also present Skepthical, a multi-agent reviewer that verifies citation-backed claims by retrieving the cited papers, mathematics with computer algebra, and numerical claims with executed code. We apply the benchmark to two corpora of 15 recent arXiv preprints each, one in astrophysics and one in machine learning (ML), injecting a total of 357 errors, and evaluate twelve reviewer systems: six single-LLM reviewers, two Claude Code configurations, three publicly available reviewers, and Skepthical. The choice of model has the largest impact on detection rate: given the same prompt, three frontier LLMs differ by about 50 percentage points in total detection on both corpora. Tools and scaffolding help when they contribute what the model cannot supply on its own. In particular, on astrophysics, Claude Code improves on a single Claude call in mathematics and numerics detection, and enforced citation retrieval gives Skepthical the highest citation error detection rate of all systems by a large margin. This citation lead, however, is not observed on ML, where the models appear to know the literature better and Skepthical's citation detection rate is statistically indistinguishable from that of the best single LLM. False-positive rates are below 10% for most systems, but vary with prompt, model, and scaffolding, with mathematics flags the main source of false positives. The evaluation pipeline, the injected errors, and the adjudication logs are released as a benchmark.

The Illusion of Diversity: Aligning LLM Exploration via Effective Entropy

Xiaoliang Fu, Jiaye Lin, Yangyi Fang, Cong Qin, Binbin Zheng, Chaowen Hu

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards has shifted the alignment paradigm of large language models from subjective preference to objective correctness, yet a fundamental disconnect persists between exploration diversity and reasoning validity. Existing strategies predominantly rely on "blind" entropy metrics, e.g., token-level or global sequence-level entropy, which structurally fail to distinguish between effective reasoning and potential hallucinations. This misalignment creates an illusion of diversity, where high entropy tends to signal chaotic degeneration rather than valuable exploration. To bridge this gap, we introduce Effective Entropy, a validity-aware entropy concept that quantifies diversity exclusively within the valid subspace and systematically filters out the noise of incorrect trajectories. Built on this concept, we instantiate VEPO, a simple Validity-aware Entropy Policy Optimization method that directly incorporates effective entropy into RLVR training. VEPO induces an adaptive attraction dynamic that drives up the probability of under-explored valid trajectories, a mechanism that matches the practical regime of large-scale autoregressive models where sequence probabilities remain extremely small. Experiments on six mathematical reasoning benchmarks with 1.5B and 7B DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen models show consistent aggregate accuracy gains over GRPO and competitive repeated-sampling success. A direct evaluation with matched correct counts finds more distinct strategies on commonly solved MATH500 problems, supported by paired statistical tests and three-seed training comparisons.

Entire sig-jump-diffusions, their expected signature and applications to score-based generative modeling

Christa Cuchiero, Francesca Primavera, Sara Svaluto-Ferro

We develop score-based generative models for time series and standard finite-dimensional data, building on the analytical computation of the expected signature of a new class of jump-diffusions, called entire sig-jump-diffusions. These are semimartingales whose characteristics are given by linear combinations of potentially infinitely many components of their own signature. We show that their expected signature can be computed by solving linear ODEs in the extended tensor algebra, providing a tractable foundation for learning procedures. Specifically, the learning of a generalized score function for jump diffusions, a key ingredient of our generative modeling framework, can be reduced to solving linear ODEs and a deterministic optimization problem. We further prove that polynomial jump-diffusions can be embedded into this framework, and that their expected signature reduces to a finite-dimensional system of linear ODEs. Building on this result, we develop an alternative approach for finite-dimensional data, based on inverse signature matching, a method that learns the score by matching the expected signature of the reverse process with that of the inverse forward signature.

Well-Balanced WE-PINNs for the Shallow Water Equations over Smooth and Discontinuous Bathymetries

Ismail Oubarka, Imad Kissami, Mohamed Boubekeur, Imad Elmahi, Fayssal Benkhaldoun

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) solve partial differential equations without a mesh, but they do not in general preserve the steady states that well-balanced schemes reproduce exactly. We present a well-balanced extension of the weak and entropy PINN (WE-PINN) framework to the shallow water equations over smooth and discontinuous bathymetry. The residual is a weak space--time control-volume identity enforced on randomly placed control volumes that may overlap, so no mesh is needed. In free-surface variables, the geometric source is integrated by parts onto the bed, so that the network is never differentiated, and a cell-local bed-slope correction makes the quadrature reproduce the exact bed increment. Bed discontinuities, located from the bathymetry alone, separate sub-domains carrying independent networks, coupled through conservation of discharge and total head. We prove that the fully discrete residual vanishes identically on the lake at rest for any control-volume family, quadrature order and nominal bed slope, so that the equilibrium is an exact global minimiser of the training objective; that the continuous residual characterises weak solutions; that the slope correction is consistent; and that no approximation with a continuous free surface can satisfy the head condition at a step with non-zero discharge, so that the decomposition is necessary. Numerically, the residual vanishes to round-off on four lake-at-rest configurations over smooth, discontinuous, compound and tabulated beds, and the trained network reaches the equilibrium to round-off under an extended training budget. On seven Riemann problems at a bottom step, covering sub-, super- and critical transitions, the network attains relative $L^1$ free-surface errors between $6.9\times10^{-4}$ and $3.5\times10^{-3}$, and reaches critical states without prescribing them.

LLM-Assisted Verification in Dafny

Wieger Wesselink, Kees Huizing, Huub van de Wetering

Formal verification of complex algorithms requires expertise in program specification, invariant construction, mathematical proof, and the management of automated theorem provers. Although large language models (LLMs) can generate program code and proof fragments, reliably developing complete machine-checked verifications remains challenging: it requires an iterative process of constructing and validating specifications and invariants, building proofs, diagnosing solver failures, and optimizing verification performance. This paper presents a systematic ten-step workflow for LLM-assisted verification of scientific algorithms using the Dafny verification system. The workflow integrates algorithm formalization, contract development, empirical invariant validation, proof construction, bottleneck profiling, and resource-driven proof optimization. We applied the workflow using Antrhopic's Claude Opus 5 and Opus 5.5 models, combining LLM-driven development with automated testing and verification feedback, while retaining human oversight for correctness-critical decisions and strategic interventions. We evaluate the approach on 37 complex algorithms from graph theory and labelled transition systems. Notably, we verified the Hopfcroft-Tarjan triconnectivity test, demonstrating that even highly challenging algorithmic verification tasks can be carried out through a structured LLM-assisted workflow. Many of our machine-checked verifications were obtained within hours to a few days of interactive development. Our results suggest that LLM-assisted workflows can shift the role of the human verifier from detailed proof construction toward supervision, specification review, and strategic guidance.

Multifidelity Operator Inference for Non-intrusive Model Reduction under Data Scarcity

Vivian Zhang, Tomoki Koike, Atticus Rex, Elizabeth Qian

Many-query analyses, in which a computational model is repeatedly evaluated, play an important role in uncertainty quantification and optimization for engineering design. However, many-query analyses can be prohibitively expensive when considering high-fidelity numerical models, especially those governed by partial differential equations (PDEs). Surrogate modeling replaces costly simulations with cheap approximations, enabling practical uncertainty quantification and design workflows. Machine learning (ML) approaches to surrogate modeling have gained significant traction in the development of surrogate models due to their non-intrusive nature; they do not require access to the underlying simulation code. The Operator Inference (OpInf) approach, in particular, fits reduced operators to state and corresponding time derivative data. However, high-fidelity training data is scarce due to limited computational budgets, and OpInf models trained on such scarce data often exhibit high variance, a critical liability in high-risk engineering design. We propose Multifidelity Operator Inference (MF-OpInf), which reduces this variance by supplementing scarce high-fidelity snapshots with abundant low-fidelity data drawn from coarser discretizations of the same problem. MF-OpInf handles data across varying fidelity levels by utilizing a multifidelity linear regression framework to estimate the reduced operators. Numerical experiments on model PDE problems demonstrate that MF-OpInf predicts PDE solution fields with higher accuracy compared to the high-fidelity-only OpInf with the same computational budget.

ControlHarbor: A Benchmark for Control Design by Coding Agents

Jieqiang Wei, Fahad Ali, Jonny Reichwald, Silun Zhang

LLM agents, i.e., language models run by a harness, can now write, run and debug software autonomously. This raises the question of whether they can also carry out control system design. In this paper, we consider control system design as a task for LLM agents and grade the designed controller as control engineers do: the controller is given in a single Python file mapping measurements to actions, evaluated on held-out seeds against an ordered set of requirements, under specified physical perturbations and structural certificates, without using neural networks. We formulate the task as a chance-constrained feasibility problem and instantiate it in ControlHarbor, a benchmark of 44 tasks over 13 plants, which includes network consensus, vehicle platooning, the MuJoCo hopper, walker and humanoid. The tasks are packaged in the Harbor format, so that any agent harness can be run against them, and the same environments also serve learning algorithms other than LLMs, such as reinforcement learning (RL), under the same evaluation. With ControlHarbor, we observe that the controllers designed by three frontier coding agents, Claude Opus 5.5, Fable 5.1 and Sonnet 5.5, reach the RL return on every parity task, but only in a minority of sessions. We also observe that the controllers certified for return alone usually fail under perturbations. When robustness is part of the specification, a structured design meets it at the RL return on the humanoid task, where domain-randomised RL does not; under large pushes, domain-randomised RL succeeds and no designed controller does. The benchmark, the controllers, and their grades are released.

Your Hypergradient is Skewed: Antithetic Neumann Estimation for Bilevel Optimization

Jason Bohne, Pawel Polak, Gary Kazantsev, David Rosenberg

Bilevel optimization is a core tool in practical machine learning workflows, including hyperparameter tuning, data reweighting, robust learning, reinforcement learning, and imbalanced classification. Hypergradient computation requires the inverse of the inner Hessian, typically estimated via a truncated Neumann series. While existing analyses focus on bias and variance, we show that the product structure of the Neumann series introduces a third source of error: skewness. We identify that, unlike ordinary gradients, whose asymmetry is suppressed by mini-batch averaging, the Neumann estimator amplifies skewness across factors, with the third-moment error growing, under weak contraction, with truncation depth, inner condition number, and per-factor noise asymmetry. This arises in many practical settings such as corrupted labels, outliers, heterogeneous or class-imbalanced datasets. To address this, we introduce the Antithetic Neumann Estimator (ANE), which pairs each Hessian sample with a mirrored counterpart around a reference Hessian and averages the resulting inverse-Hessian estimates. With an exact reference, the antithetic product retains only even-order noise, eliminating all odd-order perturbation terms; with an approximate reference, the residual odd-order contribution is bounded linearly in the reference error. ANE requires only a modification of the Hessian vector step and combines with existing stochastic or variance-reduced bilevel methods. We instantiate ANE-SGD and ANE-VR, the latter adding recursive variance reduction. Across robust regression, data cleaning, offline reinforcement learning, and imbalanced classification, ANE-based methods improve on stochastic and variance-reduced baselines on every task (20 seeds each), lowering loss by 4 to 25% relative to the best baseline; the largest gains, on robust regression, are a 25% lower test loss and a 54% higher test r-squared.

Fit-LRP: Fitting Linear Relaxations to Polytopes for Neural Network Verification over Non-Convex Regions

Brian Yarbrough, Jesse Wayment, Jingbo Wang, Philip E. Paré, Shreyas Sundaram

State-of-the-art neural network verifiers evaluate linear relaxations over axis-aligned bounding boxes. However, verification domains in cyber-physical systems are rarely boxes. Reachability specifications, obstacle-avoidance constraints, and neural Lyapunov certificates typically yield oblique or non-convex polyhedral regions, over which box-based bounds become extremely loose. This paper proposes Fit-LRP (Fitting Linear Relaxations to Polytopes), a framework the implements exact optimization of affine bounds over non-convex domains (represented by unions of polytopes) by solving a sequence of small, low-dimensional linear programs. Our approach seamlessly enhances the final optimization step of LiRPA and $α,\! β$-CROWN branch-and-bound (BaB) algorithms. In BaB verification, approximating oblique constraints with axis-aligned splits causes severe combinatorial blowup and risks non-termination. By optimizing the final affine bound of a nonlinear function over the exact polyhedral state-space constraints our method eliminates box-induced over-approximations, enabling earlier search-tree pruning. On ARCH-COMP and neural Lyapunov benchmarks, Fit-LRP reduces branching on the hardest region by $47\times$, cuts worst-case verification time by $15\times$, and with region-wise partitioning, verifies on a single CPU $6.1\times$ faster than $α,\! β$-CROWN on a GPU.

Optimal higher order convergence rates for the MUON optimizer

Thomas Beretti, Thang Do, Steffen Dereich, Arnulf Jentzen

Gradient descent optimization methods are the key tools to train deep neural networks in artificial intelligence systems. In practice, one typically employs suitable accelerated gradient descent optimization methods such as the momentum optimizer, the Adam optimizer, or the MUON optimizer proposed by Jordan et al. in 2024. In 1964 Polyak showed that, in a neighborhood of a local minimizer, the momentum optimizer converges with the rate $(\sqrtχ+1)^{-1}(\sqrtχ-1)$, which is strictly faster than the rate $(χ+1)^{-1}(χ-1)$ of the standard gradient descent optimizer, where $χ\in(1,\infty)$ is the condition number (the ratio of the largest to the smallest eigenvalue) of the Hessian of the objective function at the local minimizer. Recently, the same faster rate has been established for the Adam optimizer. In this work we show that the MUON optimizer achieves the same higher speed of convergence and we establish the optimality of the obtained convergence rates. More specifically, we propose a precise concept for measuring the speed of convergence of gradient descent optimization methods, and we establish optimal convergence rates for the standard gradient descent, the momentum, the RMSprop, the Adam, and the MUON optimizers.

Typed Knowledge over Constructive Institutions: A Proof-Relevant Type Theory for Verified Science

Hans-Martin Will

Mathematical proof assistants such as Coq, Agda, and Lean 4 build proof irrelevance into definitional conversion ($p \equiv q$ for $p, q : P$ with $P : Prop$), erasing which derivation established a theorem. While natural for mathematics over fixed axioms, judgmental proof irrelevance is incompatible with verified scientific stores, which must inspect and compare independent derivations of the same claim to assess consilience and compute counterfactual retractions when an assay, reagent, or model is invalidated. Moreover, combining definitional proof irrelevance with subsingleton elimination breaks strong normalization and renders algorithmic conversion non-transitive. We present the type-theoretic and institutional foundations of Eigenius, a verified knowledge substrate for science. First, we formulate a dependent kernel with cumulative universes ($Prop, Set, Type_k$), impredicative $Prop$, inductive families with index-forced subsingleton elimination, and intensional identity types, where definitional equality $\equiv$ is untyped joinability under an orthogonal CRS and proof irrelevance is a witnessed propositional axiom ($irrel : ΠP{:}Prop.\,Πp,q{:}P.\,Id\,P\,p\,q$). Second, we unify universe cumulativity, codomain-covariant $Π$-subtyping, and ontology class inclusions in a coercion-free subsumption judgment whose bidirectional fragments ($\mathcal{K}_0, \mathcal{K}_1$) satisfy subject reduction and are sound and complete for declarative typing. Third, we organize external verifiers into a three-level institutional hierarchy with explicit axiom policies (Adopt, Discharge, Relativize), internalize a justification logic with Why-provenance semiring support $supp(j,P)$, and embed Cooper's Type Theory with Records (TTR) to type open-world knowledge graphs. Finally, we prove consistency in Grothendieck universes and mechanize the metatheory in Lean 4 with zero sorry axioms.
