The Cost of Mismatch: Noise Amplification in Zeroth-Order Reinforcement Learning

Lianmin Chen, Junbin Qiu, Chenxing Wei, Yao Shu, Kun He

Zeroth-order (ZO) optimization has emerged as a promising approach for memory-efficient large language model (LLM) post-training, yet its reliable application to reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) remains largely unexplored. Motivated by the distinct data regimes of on-policy RL and fixed-data optimization, we develop a generic variance-theoretic framework to investigate what limits the reliability of ZO gradient estimation. Our analysis reveals that estimation reliability is structurally determined by whether the two finite-difference evaluations are performed on matched data. On-policy reinforcement learning (RL) under the mismatched-data regime produces a $1/μ^2$ oracle-noise amplification that sets an irreducible noise floor as the perturbation scale $μ$ decreases, whereas Boltzmann Targeted SFT (BOLT) under the matched-data regime eliminates this amplification via noise cancellation. We further derive convergence guarantees with explicit constants that cleanly separate the two regimes. These results naturally lead to ZO-BOLT, which applies ZO optimization to BOLT's RLVR-aligned weighted-SFT objective. Controlled perturbation-scale sweeps and matched-versus-mismatched end-to-end comparisons validate the predicted effect of data matching. Experiments across three model variants and two mathematical reasoning benchmarks further show that ZO-BOLT consistently improves over the pretrained baseline while retaining a near-inference memory footprint. The code is publicly available at https://github.com/lianmin-chen/the_cost_of_mismatch.

Embedding a de Rham Complex Structure into Eig-PIELM for Maxwell Eigenvalue Problems

Hao Kang, Liang Li, Xing Li

The perfect electric conductor (PEC) boundary condition alone does not eliminate the gradient nullspace of the Maxwell curl-curl operator. Consequently, a boundary-only Maxwell implementation of Eig-PIELM, an eigenvalue extension of the physics-informed extreme learning machine (PIELM), may retain represented gradient modes in its reduced trial space, producing nonphysical, gradient-contaminated modes. We develop dR-Eig-PIELM, a finite-dimensional structure-preserving extension that retains the linear-coefficient, boundary-null-space, and one-shot multimode architecture of Eig-PIELM. It combines compatible tensor-product grad--curl trial spaces with a constrained null-space construction to restrict the sampled PEC space to the mass-orthogonal complement of the represented gradient image before the eigensolve. Symmetric weak-form Gram assembly yields a symmetric-definite reduced eigenproblem without an eigenvalue cutoff under the stated sampling and material-positivity assumptions. For rectangles and cuboids, we establish fixed-degree exactness at the PEC vector-field space and derive rank and dimension identities. Numerical experiments show that dR-Eig-PIELM excludes the spurious eigenmodes with significant gradient components observed in the boundary-only baseline. The method exhibits rapid convergence with increasing polynomial degree and competitive work--precision performance in the tested configurations. A covariantly mapped waveguide example illustrates the potential of dR-Eig-PIELM for integration into design optimization workflows through a constrained local geometry update driven by computed cutoff frequencies and their sensitivities. This work provides a mathematical and computational framework for separating represented gradient modes from physical Maxwell eigenmodes within Eig-PIELM and supports its use in waveguide spectral analysis and geometry design.

What Drives the Zero-to-One Jump? Diagnosing and Steering Hard-Prompt Learning in RLVR

Rui Zhu, Yumou Liu, Zhenzhe Zheng, Fan Wu

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) has substantially improved mathematical reasoning, yet progress on hard prompts often stalls: a prompt can remain unsolved for many training steps before abruptly transitioning to solved--a discrete "zero-to-one" jump whose underlying dynamics and steerability remain poorly understood. We investigate two questions: what drives this transition, and can it be steered? Through event-based analysis measuring the time-to-first-positive (TTFP), we find that negative rollouts preceding successful transitions consistently retain higher early-stage generation entropy, distinguishing near-solvable failures from dead ends. This signal motivates NAPO (Negative-Augmented Policy Optimization), a method integrated with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) that ranks negatives in unsolved groups via an early-branching driver and assigns graded advantages, injecting informative gradients precisely where standard group-relative updates vanish. Under fixed rollout budgets, NAPO accelerates early solution discovery and improves Pass@256 by 10.0 points on AIME24 and 6.6 points on AIME2025, demonstrating that zero-to-one transitions are diagnosable and steerable through driver-guided negative learning. Our code is available at https://github.com/jameszhu-pixel-time/Z2O.

Controlling Sharpening in LLM Post-Training

Simon Matrenok, Nicolas Menet, Nicolas Flammarion, Andreas Krause

LLM post-training via reinforcement learning has shown consistent improvements in accuracy at the cost of reducing behavioral coverage - a phenomenon known as sharpening that can stall progress on difficult tasks. Repeated sampling objectives such as $\mathrm{pass@k}$ and $\mathrm{max@k}$ are known to mitigate sharpening, but the mechanism behind it remains poorly understood. Moreover, existing approaches for $\mathrm{@k}$ optimization use dedicated algorithms, and no general framework exists for adapting established policy optimization methods from $\mathrm{@}1$ to $\mathrm{@k}$. We derive the optimal policies for KL-regularized $\mathrm{pass@k}$ and $\mathrm{max@k}$ objectives in closed form. Our explicit solutions reveal that $\mathrm{@k}$ optimization naturally redistributes learning pressure based on prompt difficulty, directing it toward harder problems. Crucially, we show that standard $\mathrm{@}1$ objectives achieve these exact same optimal policies through prompt-adaptive regularization or reward transformation. Ultimately, this yields an optimizer-independent explanation for why $\mathrm{@k}$ objectives reduce sharpening on easy tasks while prioritizing success on harder ones. Building on these insights, we introduce $\mathrm{@k}$ Adaptive Pressure for Policy Optimization (KAPPO). Following a one-time reference calibration, KAPPO seamlessly adapts existing $\mathrm{@}1$ methods to $\mathrm{@k}$ via prompt-adaptive regularization or reward transformation, without otherwise altering their training procedures. Experiments with several RL methods on mathematical reasoning and code generation demonstrate that KAPPO provides direct control over sharpening. Crucially, we demonstrate that $\mathrm{@k}$ pressure can be fully realized with single completion training, matching the performance of multi-completion $\mathrm{@k}$ optimizers at a fraction of the cost.

ProofPatch: Reviewing Lean Statements and Keeping the Reviews Valid as Formalizations Evolve

Xujun Che, Depeng Xu, Jian Xiang, Taufiqua Khan

The kernel of the Lean proof assistant checks that a proof establishes its statement. Whether the statement says what was intended is left to human reviewers. This is the old problem of validating a specification, and it grows more pressing as language models write formal statements in bulk and agents edit formal developments continuously. We present ProofPatch, a tool that helps reviewers judge Lean statements and keeps their judgments valid while the development changes. A reviewer can ask the kernel targeted questions about a statement, such as whether a hypothesis is needed or whether a counterexample exists. Each decision is stored with fingerprints of the statement as Lean elaborated it and of the definitions it depends on, and any declaration can be pinned so that a patch changing it is blocked, whether a person or an agent built on a large language model (LLM) wrote the patch. Working on elaborated terms rather than source text is the central design choice: notation and formatting changes leave a decision intact, and a changed definition reaches every theorem that uses it. On 229 pairs of consecutive revisions of Mathlib, Lean's mathematics library, ProofPatch sends 118 of 12,898 synthetic decisions back for review. Every text-based alternative misses at least 38 of these or sends back at least 13 times as many. On 1,527 labeled statements, kernel evidence flags unfaithful ones with precision 0.88 to 0.96, and in 344 runs of an LLM agent no change to a pinned statement got through.

A Universal Policy Wrapper With Guarantees

Georgiy Malaniya, Anton Bolychev, Pavel Osinenko

Many control tasks require driving the system into a prescribed goal set and subsequently keeping it there. The considered setting assumes that a working controller is available that provably reaches and maintains the goal, albeit with suboptimal performance. This controller is referred to as the fallback policy. Conversely, although state-of-the-art reinforcement-learning (RL) algorithms can discover policies with impressive empirical performance, the neural networks at their core act as opaque black boxes learned through data-driven exploration and, as such, provide no theoretical mechanism to guarantee that the system will ever reach---and stay within---the prescribed goal set. This article presents CALF-Wrapper, a method that combines the fallback policy with a pretrained RL base policy and uses the critic estimate associated with the base policy to select between them during deployment. The theoretical analysis establishes that CALF-Wrapper inherits the goal-reaching property of the fallback policy under the stated assumptions and provides probability bounds for the settling time and the maximum distance from the goal set over a prescribed time interval. The experimental study evaluates CALF-Wrapper in four simulated control environments of different complexity. Comparisons include the base policy, fallback policy, and constrained reinforcement-learning methods formulated for the same objective. Sensitivity and ablation studies provide direct hyperparameter recommendations. The experiments show how CALF-Wrapper can provide effective task performance while attaining the maximum possible observed goal-reaching rate. CALF-Wrapper requires no additional dynamics model, online learning, or constrained optimization during execution.

POD-CNF: reduced order modeling of parametrized stochastic PDEs via normalizing flows and proper orthogonal decomposition

Andrea Violante, Nicola Rares Franco, Andrea Manzoni

Reduced-order models (ROMs) learned from trusted numerical simulations enable the real-time simulation of complex physical systems, making computationally prohibitive tasks-such as uncertainty quantification and optimal control-tractable. In particular, data-driven ROMs are opening the opportunity to learn from both numerical simulations and experimental data. However, integrating these sources of information is far from straightforward. Real-world measurements often involve multiple sources of uncertainty, including non-Gaussian noise, latent heterogeneities, unknown or hidden system parameters, and multiscale phenomena occurring below the resolution of the data. Consequently, real-world data are better described by parametrized stochastic partial differential equations, for which the development of effective ROMs remains relatively unexplored. In this work, we introduce POD-CNF, a data-driven generative ROM for efficient uncertainty quantification of parametrized high-dimensional systems. The approach combines projection-based model reduction and deep generative modeling. First, Proper Orthogonal Decomposition (POD) is employed to obtain a low-dimensional representation of the solution manifold. A Conditional Normalizing Flow (CNF) is then trained to approximate the density of the reduced coefficients conditionally on the input parameters. This formulation allows the surrogate to capture complex, multimodal output distributions, going beyond standard regression-based ROMs and least-squares predictions. We assess the effectiveness of POD-CNF through numerical experiments, demonstrating its ability to accurately reproduce output distributions while substantially reducing computational costs. Finally, a proof-of-concept application illustrates how the generative paradigm facilitates integration into Bayesian inverse problems, highlighting promising directions for future research.

Learning to evolve by matching velocities

Nicola Farenga, Andrea Manzoni

Learning the continuous-time evolution of nonlinear dynamical systems relies on trajectory-based objectives, which require simulating the learned dynamics during training and backpropagating through the numerical scheme, at a cost that grows with the rollout horizon. We show that trajectory-based objectives degenerate as the rollout horizon increases, hindering gradient-based optimization, and that the numerical scheme biases the learned dynamics by constraining the spectrum of the learned Jacobian to its absolute stability region. To overcome these limitations, we introduce Velocity Matching (VM), a simulation-free objective that relies on piecewise-linear interpolation and stochastic collocation, and approximates the velocity population risk to second order in the sampling step. We develop a theoretical framework for velocity-based learning, introducing stability estimates that control trajectory errors in terms of velocity errors, which enable us to establish universal and continuous-time approximation results when the learned velocity field is parameterized by deep neural networks. Building on a bias-variance decomposition of the VM objective, we show that its higher-order approximation of the velocity population risk yields a consistent minimizer; that it relates to the residual of an A-stable continuous-stage Runge-Kutta scheme, thus connecting VM to implicit integration without requiring any implicit solve; and that it implicitly matches the action of the learned Jacobian to that of the true one along the observed trajectories, without requiring access to the underlying velocity field or its Jacobian. Numerical experiments on nonlinear ordinary differential equations and semi-discretized time-dependent partial differential equations show that VM improves accuracy over trajectory-based objectives, while avoiding both the cost of simulation and the instabilities of long rollouts.

Nonparametric Intensity Estimation for Covariate-Driven Poisson Processes Using Deep Learning

Atsutomo Yara, Yoshikazu Terada

We consider nonparametric maximum likelihood estimation of the intensity function of a Poisson process, allowing the intensity to depend on observed covariates. Such models describe how event rates vary with external conditions, including conditions under which the intensity becomes arbitrarily small or vanishes. However, risk analysis based on the Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence typically requires the true intensity to be bounded away from zero, a restrictive assumption in applications. We develop a unified approach that avoids this assumption and derive an oracle inequality for the covariate-averaged Hellinger distance between the true intensity and the nonparametric maximum likelihood estimator (NPMLE). As an application of the oracle inequality, we establish convergence rates for NPMLEs based on deep neural networks (DNNs). Our results show that, as in nonparametric regression, the ability of DNNs to capture underlying structure leads to improved convergence rates. Specifically, DNN-based NPMLEs mitigate the curse of dimensionality when the true intensity has a compositional structure and automatically adapt to a low-dimensional Riemannian manifold structure of the event space. For intensity functions with a compositional structure, DNN-based NPMLEs achieve nearly minimax-optimal convergence rates without explicitly incorporating this structure into the network architecture. Simulations demonstrate that DNN-based estimators outperform kernel estimators in most settings, while manifold kernels remain competitive in some designs. Finally, we analyze stimulus-evoked neural spiking using data from the Allen Brain Observatory Visual Coding Neuropixels project. DNN-based NPMLEs provide better out-of-sample performance than a conditional kernel estimator, as evaluated by held-out log-likelihood.

The results show a systematic inverse relationship: models with greater self-evaluation expressiveness have weaker calibration. s1.1-7B and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct achieve the highest PVC-VUS scores, while JiuZhang3.0-7B is best calibrated, with the lowest ECE and the smallest PVC-VUS gap. Efficient Detailed Scheduling of Multiproduct Pipelines using Action-Decoupled Deep Reinforcement Learning

Kai Shao, Ling Jian, Liming Liu, Serguey V. Bogomolov, Xinmin Wang

Multiproduct pipelines transport refined products to delivery stations in sequential batches, and their detailed scheduling must meet market demand while respecting nonlinear hydraulic constraints. Retaining the full hydraulic nonlinearity yields a mixed-integer nonlinear program that is computationally intractable, while the linearization commonly adopted for solver-based optimization degrades hydraulic accuracy without resolving the heavy computational burden. This paper develops an action-decoupled proximal policy optimization (PPO) framework that efficiently produces detailed schedules while preserving hydraulic fidelity. The scheduling process is cast as a semi-Markov decision process (SMDP) with flexible action durations. To handle the discrete-continuous hybrid action space, a cascaded dual-actor architecture decouples the discrete operating duration from the continuous flow decisions, eliminating the gradient interference that destabilizes conventional coupled-action policies and thereby yielding high-quality schedules far more efficiently. Validated on a real-world multiproduct pipeline in China and benchmarked against a single-actor baseline and a linearized MILP solved by Gurobi, the proposed framework achieves delivery accuracy close to the exact optimum while substantially improving flow rate stability and substantially reducing solution time.

Thien Le, Melanie Weber

Graph neural networks are often trained on small graphs and deployed on larger ones, raising the question of which graph information must be preserved for combinatorial optimization values to transfer across sizes. We establish size-transfer guarantees for normalized graph optimization values using finite collections of energy features. For affine objectives, including MAX-CUT, coloring defect, and independent-set density, differences in task value are controlled by discrepancies in normalized scalar energy probes on all bounded-degree graphs. For general Lipschitz objectives, such as those with balance penalties, an additional representation error appears; this error vanishes on hyperfinite graph families but can remain bounded away from zero on expanders. Thus, at fixed degree bound, state space, Lipschitz constant, and target accuracy, a finite energy representation independent of graph size suffices universally for affine tasks and asymptotically for general Lipschitz tasks on hyperfinite families. These results yield prediction guarantees under explicit conditions on encoder accuracy, readout stability, and representation coverage, together with statistical generalization bounds. Theoretically, we prove Shapley-Folkman bounds with sharp parameter dependence, including a dimension-free $\ell_2$ regime, and realize the resulting energy features with a fixed-weight message-passing network using random node initialization on bounded-treewidth graphs. Experiments with exact solvers and trained models test the resulting transfer guarantees.
