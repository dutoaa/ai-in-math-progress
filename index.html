EPRO: Robust Policy Optimization Through an Imperfect Execution-Policy Predictor

Yibo Wang, Changyi Yang, Yuanxin Liu, Zhaoran Wang

RL for LLMs often separates rollout generation from policy training. Although the two numerical engines share model parameters, they can induce different policies. This creates a challenge beyond correcting a known training-inference mismatch: during learner updates, the execution policy corresponding to the current parameters cannot be directly observed. We view this as optimizing an unobserved execution policy through an imperfect predictor of its evolution. This perspective raises two fundamental questions: how can we estimate the execution policy's response to learner updates, and how much should we trust that estimate when optimizing the actual execution policy? We introduce Execution-Policy Robust Optimization (EPRO) to address these challenges. Starting from the observed rollout-time execution policy, we use training-policy changes to construct a differentiable predictor of the current execution policy, whose score yields Transported Score Centering (TSC). Our robust-improvement analysis reveals how prediction error limits the magnitude of reliably justified updates. For practical implementation, we introduce Policy Shift as an observable proxy for prediction risk and use Shift-Aware Soft Clipping (SSC) to attenuate risky updates. Across four Qwen3 model settings, EPRO achieves the highest average held-out accuracy among the compared methods on seven mathematical reasoning benchmarks.

U-Statistics-Powered LLM Reasoning

Shijin Gong, Kai Ye, Victor Chernozhukov, Xinyu Zhang, Chengchun Shi

Since its introduction by DeepSeek, group relative policy optimization (GRPO) has been widely adopted for training large reasoning models and agents, spurring a proliferation of its variants. Yet the group size, a crucial hyperparameter in these GRPO-type algorithms, remains largely chosen by trial and error. Exploiting the U-statistic structure of GRPO's policy gradient, we recast the selection of the group size as a classical statistical problem of variance ratio estimation. This problem is particularly challenging when the denominator of the ratio is small, rendering naive plug-in estimators unstable. To address this challenge, we propose a pessimistic ratio estimator that accounts for the denominator's estimation uncertainty. We characterise the statistical properties of this estimator across three sample-size regimes. Empirically, using %a jackknife-based pessimistic rule on synthetic tasks from Reasoning Gym and real-world benchmarks covering both mathematical reasoning and code generation, we show that our adaptive group size selection reduces the mean squared error of the resulting policy gradient and improves policy optimisation over fixed-group-size baselines, with modest additional computational cost.

SGD with Large Learning Rates: General Analysis & Improved Rates

Konstantinos Emmanouilidis, Ziqing Xu, Salma Tarmoun, Lachlan MacDonald, Rene Vidal

Stochastic Gradient Descent lies at the core of modern machine learning. However its classical analysis typically restricts the stepsize to be below a curvature-dependent stability threshold. Recent developments in deterministic optimization have challenged this paradigm, showing that suitably chosen large stepsizes can not only preserve convergence, but also accelerate Gradient Descent (GD). Whether this phenomenon persists in the presence of stochastic gradient noise has remained an open question (Wu et al., 2024). In this work, we answer this question affirmatively for the cross-entropy loss with linear predictors and two-layer neural networks, establishing an accelerated $\mathcal{O}(T^{-2})$ convergence rate. At the core of our analysis is a fine-grained characterization of the stochastic large-stepsize dynamics. This sharper control of the trajectory further yields tight convergence guarantees within the stable regime, improving existing bounds even in the deterministic setting. Taken together, our results show that the acceleration mechanism induced by large stepsizes survives stochasticity and extends beyond linear models. Numerical experiments demonstrate the benefits of large learning rates in practice.

The San Antonio metropolitan area has one of the highest poverty rates among large U.S. metropolitan areas. Within Bexar County, where the city is located, child poverty varies widely across school districts. We link entrants of the city's public research university, a broad-access Hispanic-Serving Institution, to their sending schools and districts, and examine their preparation scores and first-year outcomes. Across the nation's school districts, enrollment in widely taken core courses (Algebra II, Geometry, Biology) shows little relation to child poverty, while enrollment in Calculus and Advanced Placement mathematics and science is far lower where child poverty is higher, nationally and within Bexar County. Recorded between Fall 2014 and Fall 2019, these outcomes offer a reference point for change after the pandemic, test-optional admissions, and generative artificial intelligence. The analysis is reproducible; adapting it elsewhere requires local definitions and linkage validation. All results are associations among the students of one institution; establishing causes would require a designed study.

From the ELBO to Maximum Likelihood: Convergence of Variational Autoencoders for Nonlinear Mixed-Effects Modeling

Jan Rohleff, Johannes Schropp

Nonlinear mixed-effects (NLME) models are the central statistical models of pharmacometrics, where they support dose selection, individualized dosing and regulatory decisions in drug development. Statistically, NLME modeling is a marginalized likelihood problem. Variational autoencoders (VAEs) solve such problems with a neural network encoder that amortizes the approximation of the intractable posterior distribution, and allow the integration of AI components into NLME modeling. This paper provides the convergence analysis of the VAE-NLME algorithm. First, its version for the evidence lower bound (ELBO) is proven to converge almost surely to the set of stationary points of the ELBO, without assuming that the encoder is trained exactly. Second, the final VAE-NLME algorithm trains the encoder on the ELBO by gradient-based optimization and uses it as proposal for importance-weighted sufficient statistics. It converges almost surely to the set of stationary points of the marginal log-likelihood, without any assumption on the training of the encoder. Thus, it combines the fast gradient-based training of VAEs with the limit of maximum likelihood estimation.

Mohit Tekriwal, Het Mankad, Naifeng Zhang, Keita Teranishi, Sahil Bhola, Daisuke Uchida

Agentic AI tools have recently had success in mathematical reasoning. Both general purpose coding agents such as Claude Code and Codex, and specialized mathematics agents such as Aristotle, have been used for formalizing mathematical problems such as Fermat's last theorem, Erdős problems and several IMO level math problems. However, much of these efforts are concentrated in the pure mathematics domain, which benefits from relatively self-contained theorem statements, fixed mathematical representation and extensive support in existing formal libraries. Our primary contribution is a cross-layer taxonomy of failure modes and an analysis of the form of expert feedback required at each layer to recover mathematical soundness. From these observations, we derive a workflow for recurring proof patterns and practical guidance for using AI agents while preserving mathematical soundness. Our results characterize both the practical value and the current limitations of AI-assisted formalization for trustworthy scientific computing.

Beyond PINNs: Boundary-Enriched Holomorphic Networks for Laplace Problems

Andrei Vlad Nicula, Kalp Shah, Robert-Stefan Sofroni, Mihnea Andrei Bârsan, Agneya Bhardwaj, Jan Jelínek

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) solve partial differential equations by penalizing their residual, but this formulation can be difficult to optimize for solutions with singular behaviour. We focus specifically on the two-dimensional Laplace equation, whose strong analytical structure allows the governing equation to be enforced directly in the neural representation. We compare a hybrid harmonic-neural model with a holomorphic neural network whose output is harmonic by construction and therefore requires only boundary training. We further enrich the holomorphic model with singular functions chosen according to the interior angle and boundary conditions at each junction of boundary segments. On six singular benchmarks, the enriched model is the most accurate of the neural models we compare. On the four with a closed-form solution, however, that solution lies in the span of the enrichment terms, and a least-squares fit of these terms alone is exact to rounding error. On Motz's problem and the cracked beam, the neural core reduces the relative error of the recovered leading singular coefficient from about $10^{-2}$, for a least-squares fit with the same four terms, to about $10^{-6}$, and on four variants whose solution has a smooth part outside the span, the network is $4\times10^{4}$ to $2\times10^{7}$ times more accurate than a fit of its singular terms alone; classical least-squares fits with more singular terms, or with harmonic polynomials and rational poles, are more accurate still. Our results demonstrate how exploiting problem-specific analytical structure can provide an alternative to base and residual PINNs for challenging scientific computing problems.

Congestion-Aware Adaptive Routing for LLM Classification

Yuan Guo, Stefanus Jasin, Chen-An Lin

Organizations use large language models (LLMs) to classify jobs that differ in uncertainty and the cost of errors. Querying additional models may improve accuracy, but it also incurs fees and increases congestion at shared servers. We study congestion-aware adaptive routing (CAAR) policies that adapt model selection and stopping decisions to observed outputs while accounting for the steady-state congestion they generate. The objective is to minimize the long-run average cost of misclassification penalties, query fees, and holding costs. We can represent CAAR policies as a mixture of decision trees, and derive exact holding costs for first-come-first-served servers with exponential service times and processor-sharing servers with general service times. We formulate a convex optimization problem with unique optimal model loads. Congestion prices capture the marginal system-wide holding costs of additional queries and guide routing and stopping decisions through a Bayesian dynamic program. We characterize optimal stopping decisions and identify models whose information does not justify their cost. A column-generation algorithm computes optimal CAAR policies and provides an explicit optimality-gap bound. We also bound the additional cost of limiting cascade depth relative to full-depth CAAR. Finally, under a common query-depth limit, cascades with a predetermined model order can be arbitrarily more costly than optimal CAAR, even when the order is randomized and stopping decisions adapt to observed outputs. Numerical experiments distinguish the benefits of adaptive model selection and congestion-aware design.

Convergence and error analysis of problem-independent machine learning (PIML) with linear completeness

Ke Tan, Chong-Jun Li, Xu Guo

Problem-independent machine learning (PIML) is an effective computational approach that combines the finite element method with machine learning. PIML uses neural networks to learn the mapping from material distributions or element geometries to the numerical shape functions of substructures. This approach avoids repeated computation while preserving the conformity of the underlying finite element discretization, thereby significantly improving the efficiency of substructure-based finite element analysis. However, existing studies on PIML have primarily focused on numerical accuracy and computational efficiency, while rigorous theoretical analyses of its approximation errors are still limited. In this paper, we establish an error analysis framework for PIML from a classical finite element perspective, in which the numerical error is bounded in terms of the finite element discretization error and the network generalization error. Our analysis shows that, when only rigid-body displacement invariance is enforced, the resulting error estimate contains a learning-induced term that does not necessarily decrease under mesh refinement. To ensure convergence, we propose an improved PIML scheme with linear completeness. By using linear displacement field constraints to reconstruct the corresponding columns of the transition matrix, we ensure that the resulting numerical shape-function space contains the space of linear polynomials. We derive error estimates and establish convergence results of the improved scheme in the H1 and L2 norms. Numerical experiments verify the theoretical results and demonstrate that the improved PIML achieves optimal convergence rates, good generalization performance, and numerical stability.

Boiling Down Uncertainty: Deep Generative Scenario Search with Decision-Guided Evolution Strategies for Two-Stage Stochastic Optimization

Atefeh Hemmati Golsefidi, David Pisinger

One of the most common models for handling uncertainty is two-stage stochastic programming. Choosing the right number of scenarios is a trade-off between the uncertainty that can be represented and computational cost. A recent method, Deep Generative Scenario Search, generates synthetic scenarios and improves them by learning from the decisions they induce. The original approach used a black-box gradient estimate, since decisions are piecewise constant across scenarios. This paper provides the missing analytical foundation by deriving the gradient in closed form from recourse duality and an adjoint solve of the regularized reduced problem. The decision-focused direction, with a heuristic direction as a backup when duals are unavailable, defines the sampling subspace of a guided evolution strategy rather than being used as a direct update. For any guiding subspace, the expected update is a descent direction of the smoothed objective, so an inaccurate guide adds variance while the expected step still descends. For six problem families, both guided estimators outperform the baseline. The decision-focused version reduces the mean gap by three-quarters, and on the two families with the most integer variables the reduced sets beat the exact solver's two-hour incumbent in most runs, using a fifth of the time.

Interactive Neural Computers

Ross Murphy, Marcel Hedman, Gabriel Brostow

Computers and their interfaces are usually treated as fixed, so a person, or increasingly an AI agent, must adapt to whatever the operating system presents. This is limiting when someone needs a different workflow, or an interface suited to hands-free use or a disability. We explore an overlooked alternative. Rather than training an agent to operate an existing computer, we train a generative model to be the computer, predicting each next screen from past screens and the user's mouse and keyboard actions. Treating a desktop as an interactive world model raises problems that natural-video world models largely avoid. Without a camera pose to index memory, the model must remember content that has left the screen without any geometric cues. Arithmetic in a terminal has to be exactly right, not merely plausible. Small interface text must also stay legible at interactive frame rates. Our Interactive Neural Computer is structured like a Neural Turing Machine and starts to address these problems with three modules trained end to end from pixels and actions. A surprise-gated slot memory, supervised by latent next-frame prediction (MEMORY-JEPA), stores what is off screen. An exploratory query bridge brings in the reasoning of a pretrained VLM, and a residual renderer predicts only the latent changes between frames. In long closed-loop rollouts, our model recalls occluded files in 64.2% of trials (chance is 33.3%), compared with at most 56.0% for recurrent and sliding-window baselines. It also raises terminal-text OCR accuracy from 1.3% to 50.1%. Finally, because the memory is explicit, it can be transplanted at test time to causally change what the model renders.

Agentic Discovery of Simple Symbolic Inventory Policies

Xi Chen, Yuze Chen, Baichuan Mo, Jie Ying, Yuan Zhou

Designing simple inventory policies becomes increasingly difficult as operational features and system configurations expand. We develop an Agentic Inventory-Policy Discovery System based on explicitly defined simple operational formula (SOF) policy and template classes. Large language models (LLMs) revise symbolic structures, while a numerical engine fits parameters separately for each instance and evaluates the induced policies. Multicriteria parent selection, state-specific operational diagnostics, and reusable symbolic motifs guide structural evolution. A three-stage progression develops shared templates across instances and configurations with varying state dimensions. Experiments on 15,600 instances spanning 50 demand distributions cover lead times, perishability, their combination, and lead times with limited customer patience. The discovered templates improve aggregate empirical performance over evaluated SOF benchmarks and are competitive with policies requiring more elaborate online computations. For a lost-sales system with a three-period lead time, we develop an average-cost decomposition and prove that, under specified demand and cost conditions, a capped dual-shortfall specialization of a discovered policy strictly outperforms every policy in the considered capped base-stock benchmark class. This analysis links the performance advantage to the discovered replenishment mechanism. Together, these results highlight the potential of agentic discovery to generate simple inventory policies with strong empirical performance and identify operational mechanisms amenable to rigorous theoretical analysis.

Remarkably, our experiments show that these detectors are transferable across domains, although transferability is not always symmetric.

Hierarchical randomized neural networks for efficiently and accurately finding multiple solutions of semilinear elliptic equations

Jingying Hu, Jiao Li, Yufeng Xu, Jinyong Ying

Being able to find multiple solutions of a semilinear elliptic equation is a fundamental problem in scientific computing. In this paper, fully taking the advantages of randomized neural networks, we propose a new efficient and accurate method for identifying potentially as many solutions as possible. The new method is constructed from three aspects--initialization, optimization, a technique for acceleration and improving accuracy, two of which are innovative developments. Firstly, based on the basis functions of a randomized network and the sampling points, a characteristic matrix is introduced so that its singular value decomposition can be used to determine weakly variant search directions, followed by a newly developed algorithm to generate effective initial candidates. Then the good deflated Gauss-Newton method [see {\em A. Riley, et al., SIAM Journal on Scientific Computing 47~(5) (2025) A2963-A2987}] using an exponential deflation operator is adapted as the optimization to search for all multiple solutions. Local error estimate of a particular solution under some proper condition is given in terms of the neurons in the hidden layer and the sampling points for this newly designed method. Furthermore, to accelerate the computation and improve the solution accuracy, a new multilevel method based on hierarchical networks is designed. Without using any {\em a priori} information, several numerical tests show together with the multilevel method, the new method can not only accurately find multiple solutions with relative errors reaching $10^{-16}$ only in less than one-tenth computational cost for the best case, but also preserve the inherent symmetries of multiple solutions.

Online Statistical Inference for Derivative-Free Constrained Stochastic Optimization

We study statistical inference for nonlinear equality-constrained stochastic optimization problems in settings where derivative information is unavailable or expensive to obtain. By leveraging randomized sketching, the proposed method reduces the per-iteration computational cost to $O((d+m)^2)$, where $d$ is the dimension of the decision variable and $m$ is the number of constraints, making it well suited for large-scale problems. The resulting inference procedure is fully online, requires neither covariance estimation nor matrix inversion, and incurs an additional per-iteration cost of the same order as that of the optimization update. Numerical experiments demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method in terms of both optimization performance and statistical inference accuracy.
