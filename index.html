Ramanujan Challenge Results: AI-Assisted Evaluation Pipeline for Multi-Format Proofs

Alan Sorani, Ashvni Narayanan, Michael Shalyt, Tali Monderer, Rotem Kalisch, Hila Barkan

As AI's mathematical capabilities rapidly improve, meaningful benchmarks are shifting from university-level problems and math olympiads to research-level mathematics. The Ramanujan Challenge is a benchmark comprised of research-level problems in number theory, including unpublished results and open conjectures. It presented concrete formulas for mathematical constants whose correctness can be checked numerically, while their proofs may require non-obvious mathematics. Here, we analyze the Challenge submissions, measuring the relative success of different AI models, Computer Algebra Systems (CAS), and formal tools. We find correct submissions that are conceptually distinct from the official solutions, and a proof of one of the published open conjectures. Evaluating the submissions exposed a second challenge: judging complex proofs reliably at scale. We introduce an AI-assisted evaluation pipeline for natural language proofs, CAS code, and Lean formalizations that assesses semantic and logical correctness. Compared with the conventional one-shot LLM judgment, our pipeline reduces false-positive verdicts on natural language proofs from 23% to 6% and improves judgment accuracy on supporting code from 46% to 58%. This approach provides a methodology for evaluating research-level mathematics at scale, with potential applications across mathematics and AI for science.

An Adaptive Transferable Neural Network for Partial Differential Equations with Anisotropic or Sharp Solutions

Jiacong Qu, Liyong Zhu

Transferable neural network (TransNet) has demonstrated great potential for solving partial differential equations (PDEs) with high accuracy and efficiency. However, it struggles significantly when dealing with PDEs featuring anisotropy or sharp solutions. In this work, we propose an adaptive transferable neural network (A-TransNet), a novel machine learning framework that adaptively expands neurons in regions with large solution gradients to effectively capture complex solution behaviors. Instead of relying on training or random sampling, the location parameters of newly added neurons are determined by integrating the active subspaces method and local gradient analysis based on posterior gradient information. This adaptive strategy enables neurons to be allocated where they are most needed, thereby improving the resolution of anisotropic and sharp features without additional computational costs. Moreover, the shape parameters of new neurons are automatically determined from the neuron density induced by their locations, further enhancing the network representation capability. An iterative neuron expansion strategy is developed, together with rigorous analysis of stability, a posteriori error estimation, and convergence of the proposed A-TransNet method. Extensive numerical experiments on challenging PDE problems with anisotropic and sharp solutions demonstrate that A-TransNet achieves superior accuracy, efficiency, and robustness compared with existing methods.

Researcher-in-the-Loop Evolutionary Discovery of Entanglement-Assisted Quantum Code Families

Jae-Hyun Baek, Byung-Sun Won, Jon-Lark Kim

LLM-guided program evolution can produce mathematical constructions, but validity, generality, and reproducibility require distinct evidence. We develop a researcher-in-the-loop discovery pipeline that combines program evolution, exact evaluation, mathematical generalization, certified nonexistence checks, and formal proof checking. We apply it to entanglement-assisted quantum error-correcting codes (EAQECCs). Starting from tasks that did not specify a family formula, evolved programs produced finite qubit constructions whose structure researchers generalized to the family $[[n,1,n-1;n-q-1]]_q$ for prime powers $q\ge3$ at $n\ge2q+1$ and for $q=2$ at odd $n\ge5$. Lean checks the algebraic certificate underlying this family. Together with entanglement lifting, the family closes 54 gaps in archived EAQECC tables. The released evidence includes 115 verified code records, ten nonexistence results with checked DRAT proofs, family implementations, and preserved search programs for exact checks and seeded replay. In a controlled cohort of eight paired generation blocks, validation-selected iterative programs achieve success in 46.1% and 31.6% of held-out executions at unseen lengths $n=13,15$, compared with 19.5% and 3.1% for independently generated programs. In this setting, program evolution and researcher analysis turn finite computational discoveries into a proved family with independently checkable evidence.

Meshless Latent Dynamics Models for Model Order Reduction of time-dependent Partial Differential Equations

Lorenzo Tomada, Federico Pichi, Gianluigi Rozza

Latent dynamics learning has emerged as a prominent approach for Model Order Reduction (MOR) of time-dependent, parameterized Partial Differential Equations (PDEs). Traditional autoencoder-based approaches are intrinsically tied to fixed spatial discretizations during both training and inference, motivating the development of meshless methodologies. In this context, conditioning provides a natural mechanism for representing temporal evolution through spatially continuous functions, enabling mesh-agnostic Latent Dynamics Models (LDMs). In this work, we introduce a unified mathematical framework for mesh-agnostic LDMs based on conditioning in Neural Fields (NeFs). Leveraging a taxonomy of conditioning mechanisms (weak, strong, and pure), we formulate meshless LDMs as NeFs conditioned on a latent state. We establish a Universal Approximation Theorem (UAT) for this class of models and analyze their stability by deriving Lipschitz bounds for different conditioning mechanisms. Our analysis reveals an expressivity--stability trade-off, providing insight into why more expressive conditioning, despite its higher representational capacity, can potentially degrade performance during temporal extrapolation. Finally, we provide a comprehensive comparison of meshless and mesh-based architectures in the continuous limit of the spatial discretization, showing that meshless models guarantee grid-independent Lipschitz bounds, unlike mesh-based counterparts. Our experiments across Burgers' equation, Navier--Stokes flow past a cylinder, and linear advection show that meshless LDMs can achieve predictive accuracy comparable to or better than their mesh-based counterparts while using only 2% of the trainable parameters and exhibiting nearly constant error across spatial discretizations.

Here we present FARMS, a faithful and efficient foundation that transforms computational mathematics into AI-ready computing knowledge. FARMS connects formal statements to source context, algorithmic meaning and quantitative guarantees through source preparation, reusable representations and semantic review. FARMS therefore supports an affordable, reusable knowledge foundation for trustworthy automated computational science.

Experts Exhibit Structured Redundancy: Structure-Preserving Expert Pruning for Mixture-of-Experts Large Language Models

Tian Wang, Yongding Tao, Yihong Dong, Xiaoming Wu, Ying Guo, Ge Li

Sparse MoE mechanism significantly improves the trade-off between model capacity and inference efficiency. However, practical deployment still requires storing all experts, resulting in substantial memory overhead. We show that expert outputs in MoE layers exhibit structured redundancy. Across different models and task categories, the stable rank and entropy effective rank of expert outputs are consistently far below the nominal number of experts, while expert output distributions also exhibit fewer effective modes. Motivated by these observations, we reformulate expert pruning as a structure-preserving expert subset selection problem and efficiently optimize it via a greedy strategy, rather than only independently scoring and ranking individual experts or exhaustively searching over expert combinations. We propose two expert pruning methods under the unified structure-preserving framework: Projection Energy Expert Pruning (PEEP), which preserves the functional subspace by maximizing weighted projection energy, and Distributional Coverage Expert Pruning (DCEP), which preserves the distributional geometry by minimizing weighted coverage error. Both methods incorporate router-derived weights as priors, enabling them to jointly preserve the global structural relationships among experts while accounting for the local importance of individual experts. Experiments on DeepSeek-V2-Lite and Qwen3-30B-A3B demonstrate that, at the expert retention ratios of 75% and 50%, our methods outperform the state-of-the-art baselines on the majority of benchmarks and consistently achieve the best average performance across both general tasks and mathematical reasoning tasks.

Redesign often leverages simulators too complex and/or computationally intensive for traditional numerical solvers, which lack engineering knowledge and context, to ensure global solution quality. Large language models (LLMs) can interpret semantic information, including engineering knowledge about the system and changes in context, to help guide search, and recent studies have shown that incorporating this knowledge into optimization can improve search performance. However, how best to combine LLMs with numerical solvers in redesign remains unclear. We find that the best LLM-solver integration approach and the way the prior solution is leveraged depend on the structure of the design requirement change, and that the benefit of increasing LLM model size grows with the extent to which the LLM controls the search. These results show clear potential for LLMs in redesign tasks for guiding or replacing numerical solvers.

Chance-Constrained Inspection Viewpoint Sequencing for On-Orbit Servicing via Safe Reinforcement Learning

Chloé Carer, Adam Abdin

Autonomous inspection of a target spacecraft is typically the initial step in on-orbit servicing. Planning close-proximity observation viewpoints involves a complex combinatorial problem constrained by coverage, fuel, and safety considerations. This paper models the viewpoint selection as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) and applies Proximal Policy Optimization with Lagrangian relaxation to solve it. The system combines a high-level planner, which sequences camera viewpoints on an observation sphere around the target, with a low-level model that assesses each transfer using the Clohessy--Wiltshire (CW) equations for relative motion. The deterministic constraints on single-impulse reachability and $Δv$ cap are enforced directly on the action set. Safety margins are affected by navigation and manoeuvre errors, making the actual clearance stochastic. To address this, a chance constraint on the realised trajectory is implemented, with a Lagrangian multiplier governed by PID control to adapt the penalty during training. The approach is tested on a Galileo-class navigation satellite in medium Earth orbit, modelled as a point cloud where visibility from each viewpoint considers field-of-view and line-of-sight occlusion. Across various risk budgets, the trained policy maintains the keep-out violation rate within the specified limit, achieving coverage close to its maximum and staying within the mission $Δv$ budget. Compared to the usual practice of applying a fixed margin to the keep-out radius and planning deterministically, the chance-constrained policy attains less than half the violation rate at comparable coverage. This makes the collision risk an explicit design parameter, eliminating the need for re-tuning per case. The learned policy selects a viewpoint without the repeated Monte Carlo risk estimates required by a greedy, chance-constrained planner at every decision point.

Improving the Optimization Landscape of Matrix Completion with $ε$-close Surrogates

Ziye Ma

Non-convex optimization is central to machine learning and AI, yet unlike its convex counterpart, it still lacks a general and principled framework. In this work, we try to highlight the importance of combining noisy perturbations with over-parametrization mathematically under the classic setting of matrix completion (MC). Matrix completion is an important non-convex recovery problem in which only a subset of a matrix's entries is observed, and the remaining entries must be recovered using low-rank constraints. This problem is notoriously difficult because observations are limited. However, we show that by injecting $ε$-level perturbations into the nullspace of the observation mask, we can construct noisy matrix sensing surrogates whose solutions remain $\mathcal{O}(ε)$-close to the ground truth with valid restricted isometry property (RIP) constants. The existence of RIP further enables powerful tensor frameworks to be applied with theoretical guarantees. This approach reflects a broader empirical trend in which stochasticity or noise improves the curvature of the optimization landscape, allowing over-parameterized (a.k.a large) models to better separate signal from noise. Assuming each matrix entry is observed independently, we establish quantitative recovery guarantees without explicit requirements on sampling rate or incoherence, and discuss how such assumptions can further strengthen our results.

In this paper, we study the long-time behavior of finite-horizon exploratory stochastic linear-quadratic (LQ) optimal control problems as the time horizon $T\to\infty$, and establish so-called turnpike properties for such problems. In the finite-horizon setting, we derive a feedback representation of the exploratory stochastic LQ problem and analyze its long-time structure. Consequently, the derived turnpike property is novel even within the classical stochastic LQ control framework.

Exact Theory and Optimal Stepsizes for Stochastic Gradient Descent in Smooth Convex Minimization

Benjamin Grimmer, Qining Shen

This work considers the design of stepsize schedules for stochastic gradient descent in smooth convex optimization. Throughout, we assume that gradient noise is conditionally zero-mean with bounded variance. Among problems with dimension $d\geq N+1$, we provide an exact worst-case analysis of the expected suboptimality of stochastic gradient descent's final iterate. Our analysis applies to any (potentially nonconstant) schedule of $N$ stepsizes in $[0,1.5]^N$. Here, exact means that we provide a matching objective function and noise distributions where our guarantee is attained. Whenever the variance bound is at least $Ω(1/\sqrt{N})$, our theory provides the minimax optimal stepsize schedule for stochastic gradient descent and proves its uniqueness. These optimal stepsizes monotonically decrease, ruling out any accelerations in expected final suboptimality from fixed fractal schedules observed in the deterministic case.

Neural Network Approximation and Structural Complexity of Semilinear PDEs with Lower Bounds

Hayden Schaeffer

Neural networks are commonly used to learn the solution operators of PDEs such as the Navier-Stokes equations and viscous conservation laws. On standard benchmarks, moderately sized and often shallow networks achieve small errors that decay at algebraic rates with the network size. These observations are not well described by the current approximation theory, which treats the solution operator as a generic map and derives worst-case bounds over large classes of operators. We show that the structure of the equations and their properties explain the observed behavior. For semilinear parabolic equations, the approximation costs separate into two terms, where one is controlled by an invariant of the governing law and the other by the data. The constitutive law can be parameterized as a single block whose size for accuracy $η$ is $O(η^{-pχ}\logη^{-1})$ for every $p>p_f$, where the structural exponent $p_f$ only depends on the computation graph of $f$. This exponent does not depend on the dimension and is in fact zero for polynomial laws, so for the two-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations the nonlinear block costs only $O(\logη^{-1})$ parameters. The data enters through encoding layers with a bounded number of learned weights per mode, so neither term grows with the grid resolution or the time step. While the analysis only uses the max-norm error of the pointwise block, we show that one obtains approximation bounds in a stronger norm along the whole trajectory. For viscous Hamilton-Jacobi equations with a $C^s$ Hamiltonian, the approximation requires on the order of $η^{-1/s}$ parameters, and we prove a matching lower bound, up to logarithms. This shows that solution operators for this class of problems have low structural complexity, even though they are nonlinear maps between infinite-dimensional spaces.

Our main contribution is a data-driven random-feature method to obtain a parsimonious and effective function representation for Galerkin approximation methods such as gEDMD. In addition to the numerical results, we also make theoretical contributions, including a general convergence criterion for the semigroup generated by the gEDMD approximation and a counterexample showing that gEDMD does not generally preserve exponential boundedness. With our contribution, we advance the frontier of efficiently learning continuous-time dynamics from data, towards interpretable and mathematically robust modelling based on the Koopman framework.
