The balance between feature learning and collapse in dynamical systems-based generative models

Julian Brandon, Bruno Loureiro, N Alex Cayco-Gajic, Arthur Pellegrino

Over the past decade, a growing body of work has established that implicit biases in learning dynamics fundamentally shape the solutions found by neural networks, governing their learned features and generalisation performance. However, despite these strides in deep learning theory, far less is known about representation learning in generative models based on dynamical systems, from normalising flows to diffusion. Importantly, empirical evidence suggests that continuous-time generative models can be prone to mode collapse, yet a learning theory explaining such biases remains elusive. To address this, we use tools from dynamical systems and operator theory to express the gradient flow of a general class of stochastic differential equations as a composition of linear operators, allowing us to relate collapse to low-rank biases in generative models. In variational inference and flow-matching tasks, we show that this bias can manifest in two different mechanisms: i) where collapsed models are stable fixed points of the gradient flow, or ii) in sequential feature learning, where smaller modes take progressively longer to be learned, producing an effective collapse for a fixed training budget. Together these findings point to a trade-off between feature learning and collapse, suggesting an operator view as a step towards a unified framework for understanding the learning dynamics of dynamical systems-based generative models.

Deep backward multistep high-order regression methods for high dimensional nonlinear parabolic partial differential equations

Qiang Han, Shaolin Ji, Zihao Xv

We propose a deep backward multistep high-order regression (MHDBR) method for semilinear parabolic partial differential equations through their backward stochastic differential equation representations. The method combines multistep time discretization with regression targets formed by conditional expectations. Under explicit regularity, starting-value, forward-approximation and network-moment assumptions, we prove an error bound separating time discretization, conditional Monte Carlo sampling and neural-network approximation. The analysis assumes exact population minimization; finite-training-sample generalization and optimization errors are not included. For generators independent of the gradient variable, suitably constrained GroupSort networks and sufficiently large conditional sample sizes give order $J$ in the stated nodal $L^2$ error norm. The required network sizes depend on dimension, so this result does not establish dimension-independent complexity. We present a reproducible time-discretization test satisfying the analytical assumptions and three broader neural-network benchmarks, whose relation to the assumptions is discussed explicitly.

BiMuon: A Geometry-Aware Stochastic Bilevel Optimization Approach

Zhen Qin, Zhiyao Zhang, Yingbin Liang, Jia Liu

Stochastic bilevel optimization provides a general framework for many machine learning applications, such as hyperparameter learning, data reweighting, language model alignment, and actor-critic reinforcement learning. However, most existing bilevel optimization methods treat matrix-valued gradients as flattened vectors and perform optimization in the resulting vectorized space, ignoring the matrix structures common in modern neural networks. Moreover, the lower-level (LL) problem curvature can induce significant spectral imbalance in the upper-level (UL) geometry. Such limitations of existing bilevel optimization methods motivate us to develop BiMuon, a geometry-aware stochastic bilevel optimization method that integrates implicit hypergradient estimation in bilevel optimization with Muon-style orthogonalized updates at the UL. To our knowledge, BiMuon is the first stochastic BLO method with Muon-style orthogonalized matrix updates and an $O(ε^{-4})$ iteration-complexity guarantee, matching the best-known rates achieved by Muon-type methods based on matrix parameterizations. To improve computational and parallel efficiency for models with multiple matrix parameters, we further study BiMuon for blockwise diagonal matrix approximation and derive a relative stepsize allocation rule based on UL progress and blockwise LL sensitivity. We evaluate BiMuon on representation learning and blockwise bilevel optimization, providing empirical support for our methods.

BiVerify: Closing the Loop Between Backward and Forward Verification for Mathematical Self-Correction

Rui Zou, Xin Zhao, Mengqi Wei, Ji-Rong Wen

Large language models (LLMs) have made substantial progress in mathematical reasoning but still produce incorrect solutions to complex problems, indicating the need for more reliable self-correction mechanisms. Mathematical self-correction typically identifies errors through verification, which can proceed backward or forward. The two directions provide complementary verification capabilities. Backward verification checks a candidate answer against the original constraints, bypassing complex intermediate reasoning, but cannot ensure correct reasoning or objective completeness. Forward verification follows the problem conditions and current solution, enabling it to locate reasoning errors and assess objective completeness, but may overlook key constraints in complex solution chains. We propose BiVerify, a dynamically scheduled framework for bidirectional mathematical verification. BiVerify begins with backward verification to screen the answer against the original constraints, followed by forward verification of the reasoning process; subsequent direction switching, state resetting, and re-verification are governed by verification outcomes. Upon failure, the system revises the candidate or verification flow until the same candidate passes both directions. We also introduce shared mathematical tools that separate logical reasoning from precise computation. We systematically evaluate BiVerify on five challenging mathematical reasoning benchmarks using multiple mainstream DeepSeek, GPT, and Gemini models. BiVerify exceeds the strongest representative public baseline evaluated in this work by 13.5 percentage points in average accuracy, with the margin reaching 31.2 percentage points on GPT. Further analysis shows that BiVerify's overall performance exceeds that of either verification direction alone.

Zeroth-Order Riemannian Optimization on Fixed-Rank Manifolds for LLM Fine-Tuning

Xinjue Wang, Xiuheng Wang, Yejun Zhang, Sergiy A. Vorobyov, Esa Ollila

Zeroth-order (ZO) optimization estimates descent directions from function values alone, which removes the activation storage required by backpropagation and makes it attractive for memory-efficient fine-tuning of large language models (LLMs). Existing low-rank ZO methods either parameterize the additive update through Euclidean factors with a non-unique representation, or restrict each finite-difference direction to be low-rank. In this work, we propose to constrain each additive update block to a fixed-rank matrix manifold while keeping the pretrained weights frozen, so the learned update remains rank-constrained throughout fine-tuning. On this geometry, we build a Riemannian ZO gradient estimator that projects a random matrix onto the tangent space, normalizes it to correct the scale mismatch induced by projection, and further propose Zeroth-order Riemannian Gradient Descent (ZO-RGD) and its smoothed variant, ZO-RGD-S. We show that the ambient finite difference matches its retracted counterpart, the normalized estimator recovers the Riemannian gradient in expectation, and ZO-RGD converges under trajectory-regularity conditions. Experiments on LLM fine-tuning tasks show that the proposed algorithms are competitive with strong forward-only baselines across model families and tasks, with gains in several settings.

Orthogonal Updates for the Win: Towards Accelerated Adaptive Minimax Optimization

Zhiwei Zhai, Xinyu Wang, Wenjing Yan, Lei Ding, Ying-Jun Angela Zhang

Single-loop minimax methods are appealing for modern machine learning, but standard stochastic gradient updates can be unstable and oscillatory under tightly coupled primal-dual dynamics, making performance notoriously sensitive to stepsizes. To fundamentally improve stability, we develop an orthogonal update framework tailored to minimax optimization, which applies updates along orthogonalized directions to reduce update anisotropy. However, orthogonalization reshapes the optimization geometry and makes coordinating primal-dual stepsizes even more delicate. To overcome this challenge, we develop AdaSGDA, which equips orthogonal updates with an adaptive stepsize rule driven by accumulated gradient norms. This mechanism automatically balances coupled primal-dual progress under orthogonalization, yielding an effectively parameter-free method for nonconvex-strongly-concave minimax optimization. We prove that AdaSGDA avoids problem-dependent tuning while attaining the state-of-the-art $\mathcal{O}\left(T^{-1/4}\right)$ convergence rate. We further propose AdaSGDA-VR, which incorporates a carefully designed variance reduction scheme and achieves the faster $\mathcal{O}\left(T^{-1/3}\right)$ rate. Extensive experiments demonstrate that our methods deliver competitive performance compared to existing minimax optimization methods.

Fixed-point acceleration on Neural Wasserstein manifolds

Vitalii Aksenov, Sebastian Gutierrez Hernandez

We propose a fixed-point acceleration method for optimization over probability measures, applicable to a variety of machine learning problems. We represent probability measures as pushforwards of a reference distribution through a neural network map and equip the parameter space with the induced pullback Wasserstein metric. This geometric structure allows natural gradient descent to be interpreted as a fixed-point iteration on a Riemannian manifold. We accelerate this iteration using Riemannian Anderson mixing method, which exploits historical residuals to capture approximate second-order information without forming Hessians. In the present parametric setting, the manifold is represented by Euclidean coordinates endowed with a point-dependent metric, simplifying retraction and vector transport. We evaluate the method on multimodal sampling, PDE-based inverse problems, aggregation dynamics, Bayesian logistic regression, and generative modeling. Across the tested tasks, Anderson mixing reduces the number of natural-gradient iterations and achieves competitive performance relative to SGD and Adam in terms of both loss-gradient evaluations and wall-clock time. These results suggest that fixed-point acceleration is an efficient tool for improving the computational efficiency of training on parametric Wasserstein methods.

Chatter Attack: Resource Consumption Attack for Large Language Models

Yibo Miao, Yichuan Cao, Xiao-Shan Gao, Yinpeng Dong

LLMs are powerful but incur substantial inference cost, which can be exploited by malicious users to induce overly long outputs, increasing latency and operational expense. Prior resource consumption attacks either coerce repetition or suppress the end-of-sequence token by lowering its logit value. However, with autoregressive, sampling-based generation, the search space grows exponentially with length and small deviations or unstable trajectories can derail optimization, limiting attack effectiveness and stability. To address this issue, we introduce Chatter Attack, a novel class of resource consumption attacks that optimize adversarial prompts to induce predefined short target phrases, thereby avoiding search-space explosion while still eliciting long responses. Specifically, Chatter Attack triggers self-doubt using short doubt-inducing phrases as the target, and sustains this state throughout generation via inference attention loss and global entropy loss. To extend to black-box settings, we further propose a novel Bayesian Prompt Optimization (BPO) method, which models the objective function globally by constructing a discrete token kernel, efficiently exploring the entire search space using prior information. Extensive experiments across multiple models and datasets show that our method outperforms baselines, with maximum response length increases to 31.5$\times$, and revealing practical resource-depletion risks for real-world LLM inference services.

Total variation error bounds for Schrödinger-Föllmer samplers

Xiaojie Wang, Xiaoyan Zhang

Sampling from high-dimensional, multimodal target distributions plays a fundamental role in scientific computing, statistics and machine learning. Recently, the Schrödinger-Föllmer sampler (SFS) has been proposed by Huang et al. (IEEE Trans. Inform. Theory, 2025) as an efficient sampling algorithm that transports the degenerate distribution at $t=0$ to the target distribution at $t=1$, without relying on long-time ergodicity. In this paper, we establish non-asymptotic error bounds for SFS in the total variation distance by deriving regularization estimates for the associated Kolmogorov equation via the Bismut-Elworthy-Li formula. When the drift can be computed exactly, a convergence rate of order $\mathcal{O}( h|\ln h|)$ was obtained under certain smoothness conditions on the drift with a uniform time step-size $h>0$. When the drift is approximated by Monte Carlo methods, we obtain a convergence rate of order $\mathcal{O}( h|\ln h|+ 1/M)$, where $M$ is the number of samples used in the Monte Carlo approximation. This yields a sharper dependence on $M$ than the existing bound $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{M})$ in the $L^2$-Wasserstein distance in the aforementioned paper, by turning the total variation error to the weak error analysis in the setting of non-smooth test functions. Numerical experiments are presented to illustrate the convergence rate with respect to the time step-size $h$ and the Monte Carlo sample size $M$ and the effectiveness of the proposed algorithm.

On the Global Convergence of Stochastic Adam for Physics-Informed Neural Networks

Yihang Gao

The Adam optimizer is widely used to train neural networks with highly nonlinear and nonconvex objective functions. Remarkably, Adam often drives the empirical training loss nearly to zero. This phenomenon has been partially explained by neural tangent kernel (NTK) analyses of gradient descent (GD) and stochastic gradient descent (SGD). However, analyzing Adam presents additional challenges. Its first-moment momentum couples stochastic gradients across iterations, while its adaptive second-moment preconditioner depends on the same stochastic gradient used in the update. In this paper, we establish linear convergence guarantees for Adam in training sufficiently wide two-layer neural networks with either full-batch or minibatch gradients. For full-batch Adam, we construct a Lyapunov function to control the effect of historical momentum. For minibatch Adam, our analysis builds on an exact virtual-iterate representation, a conditional analysis of the dependence between stochastic gradients and adaptive preconditioners, and uniform concentration bounds for the second-moment estimates. We prove geometric decay of the empirical loss for full-batch Adam and geometric decay in restricted expectation on a high-probability event for minibatch Adam. Beyond regression, we extend our convergence analysis to physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) for solving the Poisson equation, where differential operators and separate sampling of interior and boundary points introduce additional challenges. We establish analogous global convergence guarantees for both deterministic and stochastic Adam under explicit conditions on network width and Adam's parameters, characterizing the effects of minibatch size on convergence. These results help bridge the gap between Adam's empirical success and its theoretical understanding, providing sharper convergence guarantees for PINNs than existing analyses.

Baiyu Su, Lizhang Chen, Qiang Liu

AdamW is ubiquitous in deep learning, yet its behavior remains poorly understood. We analyze its dynamics through the lens of dynamical systems and show that AdamW admits an implicit fixed-point objective: its fixed points coincide with the stationary points of a constrained and regularized optimization problem. However, not all of these fixed points are stable under AdamW's dynamics, and stability depends sensitively on curvature, weight decay, and momentum parameters. Even in one-dimensional settings, equilibria may be unstable, and trajectories can exhibit persistent oscillations rather than convergence to them. We further extend the analysis to higher dimensions, deriving sufficient conditions for local stability of the continuous-time dynamics, and propose a convergent modification that preserves AdamW's fixed points. These results clarify what optimization problem AdamW is associated with, when its convergence can be expected, and how its dynamics could inspire more reliable optimizers.

Learning inexact alternating minimization for convex problems with quadratic coupling

In this paper, we propose to learn an inexact alternating scheme in which neural networks approximate the subproblem solution at each iteration. For convex problems with quadratic couplings between the variable blocks that satisfy a quadratic growth condition, we derive a worst-case bound on the optimality gap that exposes the per-iteration error that each network is trained to minimize in a greedy fashion. Combining the bound with a PAC-Bayes argument, we obtain a fixed-horizon, high-probability bound on the expected optimality gap over the distribution of problem instances. We apply the learned scheme to robust matrix completion and low-dose CT reconstruction with dictionary regularization where we achieve a 5x-13x speedup over the inexact first-order baseline at matched accuracy. A brief end-to-end fine-tuning stage extends this up to 30x and outperforms other learned schemes. The code is available at https://github.com/paulhausner/learned-alternating-minimization.
